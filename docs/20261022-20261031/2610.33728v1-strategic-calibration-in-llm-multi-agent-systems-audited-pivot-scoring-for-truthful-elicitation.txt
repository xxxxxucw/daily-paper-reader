Abstract 

In large language model (LLM)-based multi-agent systems, reported confidence increasingly determines the allocation of scarce downstream attention, computation, and decision influence. This makes reporting strategic: an agent may over-state the relevance of its contribution even when its underlying belief is well calibrated. Existing calibration methods largely assume passive reporting and therefore do not address strategic incentives created when confidence determines downstream allocation. We formulate strategic calibration as a reporting-layer incentive problem and propose the Audited Pivot Scoring Mechanism (APSM). APSM offsets platform-controlled alloca-tion benefits, uses inverse-propensity-weighted sparse audits, charges the Clarke pivot externality, and applies a strictly proper score. Under the stated model assumptions, truthful reporting is the unique expected-utility best response. We also characterize approximate execution and the audit–variance trade-off. Calculator-free experiments show that LLM agents inflate confidence under Top-K incentives. With a shared agent-local utility interface, LLM agents reduce distortion under both Compensated Proper Scoring (CPS) and APSM. CPS achieves higher exact allocation match, whereas APSM yields lower Brier loss and numerically fewer cutoff cross-ings. Experiments further show that APSM enlarges the utility margin against allocation-changing deviations by 1.93 × and improves robustness under approximate execution. These re-sults support incentive-aware elicitation as a distinct layer for reliable multi-agent coordination. 

## Introduction 

In large language model (LLM)-based multi-agent systems, confidence is increasingly an allocative signal rather than merely a description of uncertainty. Multiple agents may contribute evidence, plans, tool outputs, or candidate answers, after which a coordinator decides which contributions re-ceive limited downstream attention, computation, or decision weight (Hong et al. 2024; Wu et al. 2023; Du et al. 2023). Con-fidence provides a natural signal for making such decisions. Once the same report also determines an agent’s exposure or influence, however, confidence reporting becomes a strategic action. An agent may benefit from overstating its contribu-tion to obtain greater downstream influence, even when the resulting allocation degrades the coordinator’s decision.

Existing approaches improve confidence quality through post-hoc calibration, prompted elicitation, or multi-agent deliberation (Guo et al. 2017; Kadavath et al. 2022; Lin, Hilton, and Evans 2022; Yang et al. 2024; Pandey et al. 2026). These methods largely treat a reported probability as a passive prediction rather than an action that affects the pre-dictor’s utility. A system may therefore produce meaningful agent-local beliefs while still eliciting distorted reports when confidence controls selection. Recent evidence that LLM agents adapt their behavior to persuasive or utility-seeking objectives further motivates explicitly modeling reporting incentives (Su et al. 2025; Agarwal and Khanna 2025). A strictly proper score does not by itself eliminate this problem when the allocation benefit remains uncompensated. Under capacity-constrained selection, a small increase in a report can yield a discrete gain in downstream influence, whereas the corresponding proper-score loss vanishes smoothly with the reporting deviation. Near hard allocation boundaries, strate-gic exaggeration may remain profitable, so calibration must account for report-dependent allocation incentives. We formulate strategic calibration as a reporting-layer in-centive problem and propose the Audited Pivot Scoring Mech-arXiv:2610.33728v1 [cs.GT] 24 Oct 2026 anism (APSM). APSM compensates platform-controlled al-location benefits, uses inverse-propensity-weighted sparse audits, charges the Clarke pivot externality, and applies a strictly proper score. Its Groves term enlarges the utility margin against allocation-changing deviations, improving ro-bustness to approximate utility evaluation. We establish strict expected truthfulness and characterize approximate execution, sparse auditing, and payoff misspecification. Experiments with calculator-free LLMs, utility-assisted reporting, and con-trolled replay in two workflows validate both the reporting failure and APSM’s allocation-sensitive advantage. Our main contributions are: 

• We formulate strategic calibration as a reporting-layer incentive problem in LLM multi-agent systems. We show that uncompensated proper scoring cannot generally elim-inate threshold manipulation when confidence determines scarce Top-K allocation. 

• We propose APSM, a new allocation-sensitive elicitation mechanism that internalizes report-induced allocation ex-ternalities under sparse outcome verification. We establish strict expected truthfulness and show that its Groves struc-ture enlarges the utility margin against allocation-changing deviations beyond CPS. 

• We separate behavioral and mechanism evidence through calculator-free LLM reporting, LLM-controlled utility-assisted execution, and controlled replay in two task-grounded workflows. The results characterize the CPS– APSM execution trade-off and show that APSM improves robustness under approximate execution even after con-trolling for utility scale. 

## Method 

We formulate strategic calibration as a reporting-layer mechanism-design problem. A calibration or deliberation procedure may produce a meaningful agent-local relevance belief, yet the submitted confidence can still be distorted when it determines scarce downstream allocation. Our objective is therefore not to recalibrate the underlying belief, but to make faithful reporting incentive-compatible. We propose APSM, an audited allocation-sensitive elicita-tion mechanism that internalizes report-induced allocation externalities under sparse outcome verification. APSM off-sets platform-controlled allocation benefits, assigns verified outcome value, prices the opportunity cost imposed on other agents, and uniquely identifies the relevance probability. We describe a generic round t; repeated deployment applies the mechanism independently across rounds.

## Theoretical Analysis 

We analyze the reporting incentives induced by the APSM. We assume fixed candidate content, additive position-weighted relevance welfare, platform-controlled selection benefits that are exactly offset by the mechanism, report-independent audits, and risk-neutral agents that maximize current-round expected utility. The scoring rule S is cS -strongly proper:

## Experiments 

We study three research questions. RQ1 examines whether allocation incentives distort confidence reports from actual LLM agents and whether compensation-based mechanisms can be executed through a shared agent-local utility inter-face. RQ2 uses controlled replay to compare reporting and allocation outcomes in two task-grounded workflows. RQ3 

isolates what the audited Groves component adds beyond Compensated Proper Scoring (CPS) under exact and ap-proximate execution. Additional analyses of sparse audits, payoff misspecification, finite report grids, and approximate optimization appear in the appendix. 

Experimental Setup 

Evaluation environments. We evaluate two six-agent envi-ronments using the same mixed population of two Qwen3-8B, two Qwen3-4B (Yang et al. 2025), and two DeepSeek-V4-Pro agents (Xu et al. 2026), with three paired seeds. Agen-tWebBench (Zhong, Shen, and Xiong 2026) contains 200 web-retrieval queries with Top-2 content-agent allocation and document-level NDCG@5 evaluation. Our MultiAgentBench database extension (Zhu et al. 2025) contains 50 PostgreSQL incidents covering ten single- and dual-root-cause configura-tions, where specialist agents communicate for two rounds before Top-2 escalation. 

Reference beliefs and execution. For agent i, qi is the frozen agent-local belief formed before mechanism-dependent reporting and is not observed by the platform. Controlled replay reuses the same candidates, evidence, outcomes, report domains, allocation rules, tie-breaking, audit randomness, and optimizer across mechanisms. Exact execution denotes exact expected-utility maximization over the finite admissible report domain. We separately evaluate LLM-controlled utility-assisted execution. A shared agent-local interface returns expected utilities for admissible reports but neither identifies the maxi-mizer nor submits a report; the LLM retains control of its final confidence. Matched-Brier, CPS, and APSM use identical instances, prompts, tool access, and parsing rules, differing only in their utility functions.

CPS and APSM share exact influence compensation and the same strictly proper score; their comparison isolates the audited realized-value and pivot-externality terms.

## Related Work 

LLM calibration and strategic multi-agent behavior. 

Prior work improves confidence quality through post-hoc calibration, prompted confidence elicitation, and multi-calibration (Guo et al. 2017; Jiang et al. 2021; Kadavath et al. 2022; Lin, Hilton, and Evans 2022; Hébert-Johnson et al. 2018; Detommaso et al. 2024). Multi-agent approaches further refine confidence through deliberation and critique (Pandey et al. 2026; Yang et al. 2024), while broader LLM multi-agent systems use collaboration, debate, and task decomposition to improve reasoning (Du et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2023; Park et al. 2023). These methods generally assume cooperative or aligned agents and treat confidence as a passive expres-sion of uncertainty. Recent evidence, however, shows that LLM agents may adapt their behavior to persuasive or utility-seeking objectives (Agarwal and Khanna 2025; Su et al. 2025). We study a complementary reporting-layer problem: even with meaningful agent-local beliefs, allocation incentives can distort the confidence submitted to the coordinator. 

Truthful elicitation and decision-dependent allocation. 

Truthful probabilistic reporting has been studied through proper scoring rules (Gneiting and Raftery 2007), Groves mechanisms (Vickrey 1961; Clarke 1971; Groves 1973), peer prediction (Prelec 2004; Miller, Resnick, and Zeckhauser 2005; Shnayder et al. 2016), and multi-agent forecast aggre-gation (Wang, Liu, and Chen 2021; Lambert, Pennock, and Shoham 2008). Closest to our model, Boutilier (2011) shows that compensating experts for forecast-induced decisions, to-gether with a strictly proper score, achieves strict elicitation under exact compensation. We instantiate this mechanism as CPS and do not claim novelty for exact truthfulness. Decision-market research further studies forecasts that affect both the chosen action and the observed outcome (Othman and Sand-holm 2010; Chen and Kash 2011; Chen et al. 2014; Oesterheld and Conitzer 2020). Our setting instead concerns LLM agents whose relevance reports determine scarce deterministic Top-

K allocation. APSM preserves this allocation rule while introducing an audited Groves term that enlarges the utility margin against allocation-changing deviations under approx-imate execution. Relatedly, Doval and Smolin (2025) study mechanism design by principals with calibrated beliefs about agent types, whereas we elicit reports from strategic agents under sparse direct verification. 

## Conclusion and Limitations 

We study strategic confidence reporting in LLM-based multi-agent systems, where confidence affects both uncertainty expression and downstream allocation. We introduce APSM, an audited allocation-sensitive elicitation mechanism that off-sets platform-controlled benefits, internalizes report-induced externalities, and uniquely elicits relevance probabilities. Calculator-free experiments show systematic confidence in-flation under hard allocation incentives. With agent-local utility assistance, actual LLM agents substantially reduce this distortion under both CPS and APSM, while controlled replay shows that APSM enlarges the utility margin against allocation-changing deviations and improves robustness under approximate execution. Our guarantees assume fixed candi-date content, risk-neutral myopic agents, report-independent auditing, and accurately modeled platform-controlled benefits. The reference belief is an agent-local proxy rather than observ-able truth, and APSM is not uniformly easier to execute than CPS. The framework does not yet address external benefits, collusion, budget balance, or strategic generation; broader strategic settings remain future work. References

## Experimental Protocol and Additional Results 

This appendix provides the complete protocol for the con-trolled mechanism replay and reports analyses omitted from the main paper for space. We maintain the same distinction as the main text: qi is a frozen agent-local reference belief formed before mechanism-dependent reporting, whereas ri

is the report submitted to the platform. The reference belief is used only inside the agent-local policy executor and for evaluation; it is never exposed to the platform allocation or transfer modules. 

Experimental Protocol 

Paired controlled replay. We separate candidate generation and belief formation from strategic reporting. For every query or database incident, we first freeze the candidate content, supporting evidence, realized outcomes, agent-local reference beliefs, admissible report domain, allocation tie-breaking, and random seeds. All mechanisms start from the same cached initial profile and use the same agent-update order and sweep schedule. Within a mechanism, reports may subsequently differ because the public utility function differs. Consequently, paired differences are not confounded by changes in generated content, task difficulty, initialization, or audit randomness. 

AgentWebBench content allocation. The content-allocation environment contains six agents per query: two Qwen3-8B agents, two Qwen3-4B agents, and two DeepSeek V4 pro agents competing in the same market. The platform selects K = 2 content agents using the additive surrogate de-fined in the main paper. Each selected agent contributes up to three documents. The documents are ordered according to the selected agents’ reports, merged, deduplicated, and truncated to the top five for document-level NDCG@5 evaluation. 

Database root-cause diagnosis. The diagnosis environ-ment is constructed from the Database Root-Cause Diagno-sis task of MultiAgentBench. It contains 50 independently initialized PostgreSQL incidents covering ten single- and dual-root-cause configurations, with five incidents per config-uration. Six specialist agents execute hypothesis-specific SQL diagnostics and exchange findings over two communication rounds. Each incident is repeated under three paired rotations of the specialist–model assignment, using two Qwen3-8B agents, two Qwen3-4B agents, and two DeepSeek V4 pro agents. All mechanisms use the same Top-2 router and the same frozen diagnoses, evidence, reference beliefs, outcomes, initialization, and tie-breaking. 

Public mechanism configuration. All compared mecha-nisms use the same public allocation values xi(a), the same platform-controlled selection-benefit schedule Gi(a), and the same normalized Brier score 

S(r, y ) = 1 − (r − y)2. (106) We set the proper-score weight to η = 1 and use a default audit probability of ϵ = 0 .3 unless otherwise stated. The ranked position values and selection-benefit schedule are fixed before reports are submitted and are reused without modification across mechanisms. The main controlled replay maximizes over a cached finite admissible report domain; the separate finite-grid ablation replaces that domain with a uniform grid of spacing 0.05 . Therefore, “exact execution” in an experiment means exact maximization over the stated admissible domain, not necessarily over the continuum [0 , 1] . Exact recovery of

Utility ties are resolved by selecting the smallest report among maximizing candidates (a fixed, mechanism-independent rule). Only the mechanism-specific utility calculation differs. Tool invocation succeeds in 100% of runs for all three mechanisms.

CPS Score-Weight Sweep 

We further test whether increasing the proper-score weight η

can reproduce APSM’s advantage under approximate execu-tion. We vary 

η ∈ { 0.5, 1, 1.43 , 2, 4, 8},

where η = 1 .43 matches APSM’s mean per-instance utility range. All conditions use the same frozen candidates, refer-ence beliefs, report set, allocation rule, and paired additive utility noise with σ = 0 .05 .Matching the mean utility range does not eliminate the gap. At η = 1 .43 , CPS retains an allocation-error rate of 17 .2% 

and a cutoff-crossing rate of 14 .3% , compared with 11 .2% 

and 8.3% under APSM. Its NDCG@5 is 0.304 , compared with 0.356 under APSM. CPS becomes statistically comparable on aggregate metrics only near η = 8 . This comparison does not imply mecha-nism equivalence: increasing η eightfold also increases the proper-score transfer scale eightfold and its audit-induced variance component by a factor of 64 under inverse-propensity settlement. The distinction is structural near a Top-K boundary. For a cutoff gap δ, CPS penalizes the minimum crossing deviation by ηδ 2, whereas APSM additionally incurs a first-order belief-space allocation loss proportional to dK δ. Consequently, reproducing the same local deterrence using CPS alone re-quires η to grow as the cutoff gap decreases.
