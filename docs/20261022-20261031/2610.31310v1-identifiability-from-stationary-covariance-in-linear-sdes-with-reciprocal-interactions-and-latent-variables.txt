# Abstract 

Continuous-time stochastic differential equa-tions (SDEs) provide a natural framework for modeling interacting dynamical systems. Acentral causal question is whether causal ef-fects among observed variables can be identi-fied from observational data drawn from the stationary distribution, given the underlying causal graph. For linear SDEs, existing iden-tifiability results are largely restricted to fully observed systems whose causal graphs con-tain no directed 2-cycles. We first establish new graphical conditions for the generic iden-tifiability of causal effects in systems that may contain directed 2-cycles, thereby allow-ing for reciprocal interactions. Next, we ad-dress the previously unexplored setting of la-tent variables by developing an instrumental-variable framework for identifying a target di-rect causal effect under partial observability. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Understanding causal interactions in dynamical sys-tems is a fundamental problem across biology (Mar-bach et al., 2012; Lorch et al., 2026), economics (Hamilton, 2020), and engineering (˚ Astr¨ om, 2012). Stochastic differential equations (SDEs), in which the diffusion captures external stochastic perturbations and the drift function describes the deterministic in-teractions among variables, are a natural model for many such systems that evolve continuously over time (Øksendal, 2003). Recovering the drift coefficients shows the local direct causal effects controlling the sys-tem dynamics. However, temporal trajectories are ei-ther not available or only partially observed in many applications. Instead, data consist of samples collected from a system operating near equilibrium. In this con-text, stationary SDEs have emerged as an attractive framework for causal modeling (Varando and Hansen, 2020; Lorch et al., 2024). Under standard stability assumptions, stationary lin-ear SDEs satisfy the continuous Lyapunov equation, which provides an algebraic relationship between the stationary covariance matrix and the unknown drift matrix. This connection enables causal information to be inferred from equilibrium observations without re-quiring access to the underlying trajectories. Further-more, graphical continuous Lyapunov models provide a causal graphical representation analogous to struc-tural causal models while naturally accommodating cycles and self-loops, common features in real dynami-cal systems (Varando and Hansen, 2020; Hansen, 2025; Fitch, 2019). In linear SDEs, a fundamental challenge is whether the drift matrix can be recovered from the station-ary covariance matrix when its sparsity pattern is known. Recent work has shown that the drift ma-trix is globally identifiable when the underlying graph is simple, i.e., when it contains no directed cycles of length two (Dettling et al., 2023). Reciprocal inter-actions, therefore, constitute the principal obstruction to identifiability. Directed 2-cycles naturally arise in many applications. In biological regulatory networks, for example, mutually activating and mutually repress-ing components form fundamental feedback motifs re-sponsible for bistability and switch-like behavior (Fer-rell Jr, 2002). Reciprocal interactions between corti-cal and subcortical are also ubiquitous in neural cir-cuits (Cruz et al., 2023). Similar bidirectional inter-actions are also present in engineered systems with coupled controllers and feedback loops (˚ Astr¨ om and Murray, 2021). Whether directed 2-cycles necessarily prevent identification, or whether additional graphical structure can resolve the resulting ambiguity, remains largely unexplored. Another challenge arises when the system is only par-tially observed. In many applications, latent variables directly influence the variables of interest, introducing confounding that prevents direct causal effects from being recovered through standard adjustment tech-niques. Although latent confounding has been ex-tensively studied in structural causal models through arXiv:2610.31310v1 [cs.LG] 22 Oct 2026 Identifiability from Stationary Covariance 

instrumental variables, adjustment criteria Perkovi´ cet al. (2018), and do-calculus Pearl (2009), comparable findings for stationary SDEs are far less established. In this paper, we study identification from stationary covariance in non-simple linear SDEs through a com-mon rank-based perspective. Directed 2-cycles are al-lowed throughout. Under full observability, we study graphical conditions that guarantee identification of the complete drift matrix through a global rank con-dition derived from the stationary Lyapunov equation. Under partial observability, latent variables prevent full drift recovery, but suitable instruments lead to a target-row rank condition for identifying coefficients of interest. Both settings follow the same principle: us-ing graphical structure to certify rank conditions for covariance-based systems induced by the stationary Lyapunov equation. Our main contributions are: 

• We establish new graphical conditions for generic identifiability of graphical continuous Lyapunov models containing directed 2-cycles (Theorem 1). We further characterize several identifiable four-node motifs that fall outside these condi-tions (Proposition 1) and show how identifiability can be localized to identifiable subgraphs within larger non-identifiable systems (Theorem 2); 

• We provide an instrumental-variable framework for identifying direct drift coefficients under la-tent confounding using only observed covariance matrix (Theorem 3); 

• We provide graphical conditions guaranteeing the full-rank requirement of the instrumental covari-ance matrix. We completely characterize the direct-instrument case via a bipartite match-ing condition (Theorem 4) and derive a suffi-cient graphical criterion based on vertex-disjoint directed paths when intermediate variables are present (Theorem 5). 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

Causal modeling with stochastic differential equations. Classical causal inference is predomi-nantly formulated through structural causal models (Pearl, 2009). Extending causal reasoning to dynami-cal systems requires accounting for temporal dynamics and feedback. Hansen and Sokol (2014) formalized in-terventions on stochastic differential equations, while Bongers and Mooij (2018); Mogensen et al. (2018) de-veloped causal models for general stochastic dynamical systems. When temporal trajectories are unavailable, inference may rely on stationary observations. Initial work investigated learning directed graphical models from stationary Gaussian observations (Fitch, 2019), while Varando and Hansen (2020) introduced graphical con-tinuous Lyapunov models, in which the sparsity pat-tern of the drift matrix defines a directed graph. In this representation, a directed edge corresponds to a direct interaction in the drift, while the stationary co-variance summarizes the equilibrium dependence in-duced by the entire dynamical system. Subsequent work established a trek rule providing a graphical char-acterization of stationary covariance entries (Hansen, 2025), while Lorch et al. (2024, 2026) developed meth-ods for causal learning directly from stationary distri-butions. A fundamental question is when the drift matrix is identifiable from stationary covariance information. Dettling et al. (2023) proved that graphical continuous Lyapunov models are globally identifiable if and only if the underlying graph is simple, and further estab-lished necessary conditions for generic identifiability. Our work complements this line of research by iden-tifying graphical conditions under which non-simple graphs remain generically identifiable and by charac-terizing local graphical structures that repair the am-biguity introduced by directed reciprocal interactions. 

Causal effects under partial observation Par-tial observation is a central challenge in causal in-ference where unobserved common causes can inval-idate adjustment, induce spurious associations, and prevent causal effects from being recovered from the observed distribution alone. In structural causal mod-els, valid adjustment set or do-calculus provide solu-tions to identify causal effects in the presence of la-tent variables (Pearl, 2009; Richardson and Spirtes, 2002; Shpitser and Pearl, 2006; Shpitser et al., 2012; Perkovi´ c et al., 2018). However, these approaches are mainly developed for acyclic structural causal models where no directed cycle is allowed. In linear structural equation models, generic identifi-ability from observational covariance has been stud-ied through graphical rank criteria, including the half-trek criterion (Foygel Barber et al., 2022), instrumen-tal cutsets (Kumor et al., 2019), and latent-variable extensions (Foygel et al., 2012). These approaches, like ours, use graphical path structures to certify rank conditions. Our setting differs in that identification is based on stationary covariance constrained by the continuous Lyapunov equation. For stationary SDEs, considerably less is known. Ex-isting work primarily focused on recovering the com-plete drift matrix under full observation (Dettling et al., 2023). In many applications, however, recov-ering the entire dynamical system is unnecessary; in-Ana Let´ ıcia Garcez Vicente, Saber Salehkaleybar 

stead, the objective is to identify a particular direct causal effect despite latent confounding. More re-cently, van Seeventer and Salehkaleybar (2026) investi-gated sign identifiability from stationary second-order information, including settings with latent variables. Additionally, instrumental processes whose identifying moment equations are built from integrated covariance information have been used to extend instrumental-variable approaches to stochastic processes observed over time (Mogensen, 2023). Our second contribution differs from these approaches. Rather than recover-ing the entire drift matrix, identifying only the sign of causal effects, or relying on trajectory observations to estimate integrated covariances, we study identifica-tion of a target drift coefficient under latent confound-ing. We derive instrumental equations and establish graphical conditions under which the observed station-ary covariance uniquely identifies the target causal ef-fects. 

# 3 PRELIMINARIES 

3.1 Stochastic differential equations 

Stochastic differential equations (SDEs) provide a nat-ural framework for modeling continuous-time systems evolving both deterministic interactions and random perturbations indexed by node set V , with |V | = d.Let X(t) ∈ Rd denote the state vector at time t. Each component Xv (t) represents variable v ∈ V whose evo-lution is influenced by other variables in the system as well as by external stochastic perturbations. Our anal-ysis focuses on the linear SDEs: 

dX(t) = MX (t)dt + DdW(t), (1) which corresponds to the multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process. D is the diffusion matrix and W(t) is a d-dimensional standard Brownian mo-tion. The matrix M = ( mij )i,j ∈V ∈ Rd×d is the drift matrix and characterizes the deterministic interactions among variables. In particular, the entry mij quanti-fies the direct effect of variable Xj on the evolution of 

Xi. Therefore, from a causal perspective, mij repre-sents the direct causal effect from Xj to Xi.Let C := DD ⊤. Under the stability condition that all eigenvalues of M have strictly negative real part, the process admits an unique stationary Gaussian distri-bution with covariance matrix Σ satisfying: 

MΣ + ΣM ⊤ + C = 0 . (2) Following Dettling et al. (2023), we assume that the diffusion matrix C is known. 

3.1.1 Graphical Representation 

The drift matrix can be associated with a directed graph G = ( V, E ), called the drift graph. Each node in 

V corresponds to one component of the stochastic pro-cess X = ( X1, · · · , X d), and a directed edge Xi → Xj

is present whenever the drift coefficient describing the influence of Xi on Xj is nonzero, i.e., mji ̸ = 0. The variables are partitioned into V = O ∪ L, where O

denotes the observed variables, and L are the unob-served/latent ones. For a node x ∈ V , we denote the set of parents of x

Pa( x) = {v ∈ V : v → x} the set of nodes with a direct edge pointing to x. Similarly, the set of children of x

is Ch( x) = {v ∈ V : x → v}. A directed path from a node x to y (x ⇝ y) is a sequence of distinct nodes x =

v0 → · · · → vk = y, such that every consecutive pair of nodes is connected by a directed edge oriented in the same direction. The ancestors of x, denoted by An( x), are the nodes from which there exists a directed path to x, including x itself. Conversely, the descendants, De( x), are the nodes reachable from x through directed paths, including x.The graph G may contain directed cycles. A di-rected cycle of length k is a sequence of distinct nodes 

v1 → · · · → vk → v1, with k ≥ 2. In particular, a cy-cle of length two, referenced as a 2-cycle throughout this paper, consists of two reciprocal edges x → y and 

y → x representing mutual feedback. A graph with-out directed cycles is called a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG). Our framework does not assume acyclicity. Under the stability assumption, the stationary distri-bution of the linear SDE driven by a Wiener process is multivariate Gaussian. Without loss of generality, we assume that the stationary mean is zero. Conse-quently, the stationary distribution is completely char-acterized by its covariance matrix, and our identifia-bility analysis can therefore be formulated entirely in terms of stationary covariances. A trek between two nodes x and y is a path without colliders, i.e., a path that can be decomposed into two directed paths sharing a common source node c, x ←· · · ← c → · · · → y. The node c is called the top of the trek. A trek may degenerate into a purely directed path if c = x or c = y. Treks play a central role in OU models with Wiener process. Each entry ( i, j ) of stationary covariance admits a decomposition as sums of contributions over treks connecting the pair ( i, j ). Therefore, the existence or absence of treks determines whether two variables may exhibit nonzero covariance. In particular, if no trek connects two nodes Xi and Xj ,then Σ ij = 0, implying that Xi and Xj are marginally independent (Boege et al., 2025). Identifiability from Stationary Covariance

# 4 GENERIC IDENTIFIABILITY UNDER FULL OBSERVABILITY 

In this section, we investigate generic identifiability of the drift matrix under full observability. We first characterize a class of non-simple graphs that remain generically identifiable despite containing directed 2-cycles (Theorem 1). We then show a class of 4-node motifs generically identifiable (Proposition 1). 

4.1 Identifiability Beyond Simple Structures 

Generic identifiability of linear stochastic differential equations has been extensively studied through the algebraic characterization of the Lyapunov equation 2. Dettling et al. (2023) showed that the drift ma-trix is globally identifiable if and only if the graph is simple, i.e., contains no 2-cycles. They also observed that certain non-simple graphs may remain generically identifiable, illustrating this through isolated exam-ples. However, no general graphical characterization of such non-simple identifiable graphs was provided. The goal of this section is to characterize a class of non-simple graphs for which generic identifiability is guaranteed.

4.2 Four-Node Non-Simple Identifiable Motifs 

Theorem 1 provides a sufficient condition for generic identifiability by requiring that every directed 2-cycle has a neighboring node adjacent to exactly one of its nodes. This condition is not necessary. We now inves-tigate four-node graphs that violate this condition yet remain generically identifiable. Identifiability in the presence of a direct 2-cycle is not governed by adjacency alone. A common parent, a common child, an asymmetric directed path, or an ad-ditional 2-cycle may still fail to repair the rank defi-ciency created by a 2-cycle. What matters is how the trek structure of the graph constrains the covariance entries in the matrix H(Σ)Ec,·. By Lemma 1, when

5.2.3 Bottleneck Investigation 

Theorem 5 gives a sufficient, but not necessary, graph-ical condition for generic full rank of ΣP Z based on vertex-disjoint paths. We investigate whether recov-ery remains possible when the number of intermediate nodes is insufficient to satisfy this condition. For each graph configuration, we generate 100 ran-dom stable drift matrices with diagonal diffusion ma-trices and compute the stationary covariance matrix directly from the Lyapunov equation. The generated graphs have a layered structure in which every directed path from Z to P ′ passes through the set Q of inter-mediate nodes, with no direct edges from Z to P ′.Hence, whenever |Q| < |P ′| − 1, the |P ′| − 1 pairwise vertex-disjoint paths required by Theorem 5 cannot exist. # 7 CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 

We studied identification from stationary covariance in non-simple linear SDEs through a common rank-based perspective. Directed 2-cycles are allowed in both the fully and partially observed settings. We es-tablished graphical conditions for generic identifiabil-ity in the presence of directed 2-cycles, developed an instrumental-variable framework for identifying direct drift coefficients under latent confounding, and derived graphical conditions guaranteeing the required instru-mental covariance rank. As this work focuses on the theoretical problem of identifiability, an important di-rection for future work is to investigate the statisti-cal properties of the proposed framework under finite samples, including consistency, convergence rates, and robustness to covariance estimation error. Additional directions include extending the graphical identifiabil-ity results to broader classes of reciprocal structures and relaxing assumptions on the diffusion matrix. B.2 Embedding the Motifs in Larger Graphs 

The identifiable configurations above need not occur as isolated four-node graphs. The same block-rank argument can be used when several such motifs are embedded in a larger graph. Then the same block-rank construction used in the proof of Theorem 1 extends to the full graph.
