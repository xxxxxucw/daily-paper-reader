## ABSTRACT 

Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLMs) generate sequences by iteratively replacing masked tokens with model predictions. At each denoising step, the decoder chooses which positions are sufficiently confident to commit. Existing decoding methods typically rely on softmax confidence, which can be miscali-brated. We introduce B AYES ER ( BAYES ian Entropy-based Reordering), a post-hoc Bayesian decoding framework that uses predictive uncertainty to guide to-ken commitment. In B AYES ER, we construct a lightweight approximate poste-rior centered at the pretrained checkpoint, similar to Laplace-LoRA but without training LoRA adapters. We average predictions over posterior samples and use predictive entropy to prioritize reliable positions. We examine how posterior pre-dictions affect position ordering and token selection across benchmarks spanning code generation, mathematical reasoning, planning, and molecular generation. We show that B AYES ER reduces sequence-level calibration error while preserving or improving accuracy relative to common decoding schemes, including confidence-threshold decoding. Additionally, a posterior fitted on one code-generation dataset reduces calibration error on another without refitting, suggesting that Bayesian un-certainty may provide a transferable signal for more reliable MDLM decoding. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Language models can make wrong predictions with high confidence. This failure is relatively preva-lent, as users often mistake stylistic confidence for evidence without justified certainty. A confidence score should mean what it claims: predictions with confidence q should be correct about a fraction q

of the time; this is known as calibration . Formally, if the model M is perfectly calibrated, the follow-ing holds: P(M′s prediction is correct | M′s confidence is q) = q, according to Guo et al. (2017). This definition of calibration does not eliminate hallucinations or reasoning errors common in mod-ern language models. However, it makes uncertainty actionable, enabling a user or downstream system to decide whether to trust an answer, verify it, defer to a human, or abstain. Calibration in masked diffusion language models (MDLMs) plays an unusually direct role. Unlike autoregressive models that generate tokens from left to right, MDLMs iteratively denoise a partially masked sequence. At each step, a bidirectional forward pass predicts a distribution over the vo-cabulary at every masked position, and the decoder selects a subset of these predictions to commit before continuing. This process enables parallel generation, arbitrary-order decoding, and infilling, and has recently scaled to models such as LLaDA and Dream (Nie et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025). Consequently, confidence serves two distinct purposes in MDLMs: it should indicate whether the final sequence is likely correct (the usual confidence in the model’s prediction), and more interest-ingly, it can guide which token predictions are reliable enough to commit at a given time-step during generation. This creates a central decoding problem that does not arise in standard autoregressive generation: Which masked positions should be committed at each denoising step? 

Committing many positions in parallel reduces model evaluations, but once committed, those tokens provide context for all subsequent tokens being unmasked at later denoising steps. Committing too conservatively avoids such problems but reduces the computational advantage of parallel decod-ing. The token commitment ordering therefore controls both generation quality and inference cost. Existing decoders commonly commit to the tokens at certain positions with the largest top-token 1arXiv:2610.05125v1 [cs.LG] 4 Oct 2026 probabilities or all positions whose confidence exceeds a fixed threshold (Chang et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2025). These rules are efficient and often effective, but they rely on the softmax distribution of a single parameter estimate. Neural networks are often overconfident (Guo et al., 2017), and point-estimate confidence does not reveal whether a prediction remains stable under model uncertainty. In this paper, we investigate whether Bayesian predictive uncertainty provides a better signal for commitment ordering. Given an approximate posterior q(θ|D ) over model parameters θ, the pos-terior predictive distribution over the output y of a model averages token probabilities across pos-terior samples, given a noisy state xt of the denoising process: ¯p(y|xt) = Eθ∼q(θ|D ) [p(y|xt, θ)] .

Its entropy, H(¯ p(y|xt)) , measures the total uncertainty of the Bayesian prediction. Our method, BAYES ER ( BAYES ian Entropy-based Reordering), is a post-hoc decoding framework for MDLMs that uses Bayesian predictive entropy. At each denoising step, B AYES ER forms a posterior predic-tive distribution for every currently masked position, prioritizes positions with low posterior pre-dictive entropy, selects tokens from the posterior predictive mean, and uses posterior confidence to determine how many positions to commit. In this way, B AYES ER adjusts both the order and pace of decoding based on the Bayesian predictive distribution. For typical MDLMs such as LLaDa (Nie et al., 2025) and Dream (Ye et al., 2025), with 7 bil-lion parameters or more, identifying posterior distributions over all parameters is computationally challenging. Laplace-LoRA first fine-tunes LoRA adapters to obtain a task-specific maximum a posteriori solution and then approximates a posterior around the adapted model (Yang et al., 2024). While appropriate for downstream adaptation, this procedure changes the mean predictor and makes it difficult to distinguish the benefit of Bayesian uncertainty from that of fine-tuning. We instead con-struct a curvature-only Laplace-LoRA posterior directly at the released checkpoint , without adapter training . The LoRA update is zero at the posterior mean, so the deterministic model is unchanged by construction. We fit the posterior covariance with training data, representing each block of a Hessian matrix using a Kronecker product of input and output-gradient second moments to reduce the cost of storing and expressing curvature. Across several benchmark datasets spanning code generation, mathematical reasoning, plan-ning, and molecule generation, B AYES ER substantially reduces expected calibration error (ECE) while preserving—and in several settings improving—task accuracy relative to commonly used confidence-based decoding schemes. Our results show that prior precision and the number of Laplace-LoRA layers govern a three-way trade-off among calibration, task performance, and de-coding cost. We further find that a posterior constructed on one code-generation dataset can reduce calibration error on another without refitting, hinting that the learned uncertainty signal may transfer across related tasks.

## 3 METHOD : B AYES ER 

A decoder in MDLMs makes three decisions at every denoising step: which positions to commit ,

which tokens to write , and how many positions to commit . B AYES ER makes all three decisions from a checkpoint-centered posterior predictive distribution. Specifically, it ranks positions by posterior predictive entropy, selects token identities from the posterior predictive mean, and determines the commitment budget from posterior confidence. BAYES ER consists of two stages: (1) First, we fit the posterior distribution over LoRA adapter parameters using training data, from which we obtain the posterior samples; and (2) Then, using the posterior samples, we compute the predictive posterior entropy at every denoising step t to determine 3how many tokens to unmask and which positions to fill with which token. ## 4 RELATED WORK 

Decoding and unmasking policies. MaskGIT and Fast-dLLM (Chang et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2025) present confidence-based decoders ; Deferred Commitment Decoding adapts the active win-dow (Shu et al., 2026), and Entropy based sampler uses a point estimate of entropy to control parallel commitments (Ben-Hamu et al., 2025). Lookahead Unmasking and the Information-Gain (Info-Gain) Sampler evaluate candidate successor states (Lee et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2026). Other approaches learn unmasking policies through reinforcement learning (Jazbec et al., 2026) or de-rive ordering corrections from terminal rewards, as in DPRM (Bu et al., 2026). None of the above methods uses posterior predictive uncertainty to choose positions, tokens, and commitment counts.

Building on structured Laplace approximations (Ritter et al., 2018; Daxberger et al., 2021), our curvature-only construction fits uncertainty around zero-update adapters at the released check-point. ## 5 EXPERIMENTS 

5.1 SETUP 

We evaluate Dream-v0-Instruct-7B (Ye et al., 2025) on code generation (MBPP, HumanEval), math-ematical reasoning (GSM8K, MATH500), and planning (4 ×4 Sudoku, Countdown). HumanEval transfers the MBPP posterior without refitting. We also include LLaDA results in Appendix D. We additionally evaluate molecular scaffold decoration with GenMol V2. Detailed configurations are available in Appendix A.2. We compare against confidence-threshold decoding, Base Entropy, and Info-gain (Yang et al., 2026). Info-gain runs use N = 8 candidate paths with fixed K = 2 tokens per step. We report task accuracy and sequence-level calibration. 5.2 MAIN RESULTS 

5.2.1 MATHEMATICAL REASONING AND PROGRAMMING 

Table 1 shows that B AYES ER improves sequence-confidence calibration on several tasks, with task-dependent changes in accuracy. On MBPP, the accuracy-oriented setting approximately preserves threshold accuracy (57.53% versus 57.60%) while reducing generated-content ECE from 0.2918 to 0.2354. The calibration-oriented setting lowers ECE further to 0.1632 at 56.73% accuracy. Trans-ferring the MBPP posterior to HumanEval also reduces ECE and Brier scores without refitting: generated-content ECE falls from 0.3090 to 0.2016 in the calibration-oriented setting, with 55.08% accuracy versus 54.88% for threshold. Base Entropy retains the highest HumanEval accuracy at 57.32%. On GSM8K, Base Entropy achieves the highest accuracy (78.17%) and lowest generated-content Brier score, while the calibration-oriented B AYES ER setting achieves the lowest ECE in both scopes. On MATH500, B AYES ER improves accuracy from 42.40% to 43.53% and reduces ECE and Brier scores. The accuracy–calibration tradeoff will be further elaborated in Section 5.3. 5.2.2 PLANNING 

Table 2 shows contrasting outcomes on the two planning tasks. Sudoku offers little advantage for Bayesian decoding: threshold and Base Entropy reach 97.60% accuracy, and neither posterior set-ting improves on threshold’s Brier score. Countdown exhibits the strongest accuracy–calibration trade-off. The accuracy-oriented B AYES ER setting reaches 32.13% accuracy, exceeding threshold (18.90%) and Base Entropy (23.90%), but falling below Info-gain (41.47%). Its generated-content ECE and Brier score are lower than Info-gain’s: 0.2898 versus 0.4563 and 0.2671 versus 0.4240, respectively. The calibration-oriented setting further reduces generated-content ECE to 0.2570 at 27.80% accuracy. 5.2.3 MOLECULAR GENERATION 

On GenMol V2 scaffold decoration (Table 3), B AYES ER achieves the highest mean quality yield at 15.33%, compared with 11.78% for native GenMol and 14.89% for Base Entropy. Its ECE is lower than native GenMol’s (35.36 versus 45.08 on the table’s scale), but nearly identical to Base Entropy’s (35.40). We additionally evaluate how prior precision λ, the number of uncertain trailing layers Ls, and the commitment budget jointly affect the accuracy–calibration tradeoff. In Figure 1, each panel shows five prior precisions ( λ ∈ { 100 , 200 , 300 , 500 , 1000 }) at four depths. Intermediate precision provides operating points with lower ECE and comparable or higher accuracy. Further weakening the prior can reduce ECE while substantially degrading accuracy. Depth and commitment budget also interact. A single uncertain layer reaches 59.0% at ECE 0.223 for τB = 0 .85 , λ = 300 , and 58.4% at ECE 0.147 for τB = 0 .90 , λ = 200 . These examples show that a shallow posterior can offer a favorable accuracy–calibration trade-off and that additional uncertain layers do not consistently improve it. 5.4 COMPUTATIONAL EFFICIENCY 

Figure 2 shows HumanEval cost on A100 GPUs with S = 10 samples and τB = 0 .90 . Across tested priors, increasing uncertain depth from one to four layers raises evaluation time from 17.4–22.1 to 28.8–52.9 minutes, versus 13.4 minutes for threshold. Peak allocated memory remains within 15.54–15.71 GiB, versus 14.38 GiB for threshold. Deeper posteriors require longer sampled for-ward passes, while weaker priors can prolong decoding. Shallow posteriors therefore limit runtime overhead. Memory varies little with depth. 

## 6 CONCLUSION AND DISCUSSION 

We introduced B AYES ER, a post-hoc Bayesian decoding scheme that uses posterior predictive un-certainty to guide position ordering, token selection, and commitment budgets in masked diffusion language models. Its curvature-only Laplace-LoRA construction preserves the released checkpoint at the posterior mean and requires no adapter fine-tuning. The experiments show that posterior prediction can improve sequence-confidence calibration while preserving or improving task perfor-mance at selected operating points. In future work, we plan to extend the single Gaussian posterior to multiple Gaussian posteriors across different adapter-parameter layers to better capture parameter uncertainties and enhance our entropy-based decoding scheme. 9REPRODUCIBILITY STATEMENT
