# Abstract 

External causal reports can improve structure learning from limited observations, but their reliability varies across sources and variable pairs. We introduce HB-NoisyKG , a Bayesian framework that combines observational data with repeated causal reports from sources such as large language models. Each report is a noisy observation of a direct pair state implied by one DAG. A feature-conditioned Beta prior pools information about pair reliability, and a shared error matrix captures systematic mistakes. Alternating inference uses the graph posterior to refine reliability estimates, which determine how reports influence subsequent graph updates. The report likelihood uses only graph pair-state marginals, so the same observation layer supports discrete and continuous likelihoods in graph-only and joint inference. Against an 80-restart no-KG baseline, HB uses at most 80 total restarts and lowers mean SHD from 22 .39 to 16 .06 on five discrete benchmarks. On a physical light tunnel with random variable IDs and retained descriptions, HB lowers SHD from 39 .00 for no-KG to 27 .30 . On continuous Sachs, graph-only BGe raises AUROC by .121 over no-KG Top-K. In a controlled synthetic study, continued updating also reduces mean reliability estimation error and held-out report log loss compared with one-time estimation. 

# 1 Introduction 

Researchers often have partial causal information from pathway databases, mechanistic reports, the literature, or large language models (LLMs). These sources can help recover structure from limited observations, but the quality of their claims varies. Some reports identify a direct relation, others reverse its direction, and still others describe an influence mediated by another measured variable. Repeated queries provide additional evidence, including disagreements and abstentions. The practical task is to combine this information with data and estimate how much influence each reported relation should have. Bayesian structure learning represents uncertainty over directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) [24, 9, 22]. It supports model averaging for causal quantities and posterior-based intervention design [31], but inference is difficult because the graph space is combinatorial. External knowledge can guide this inference. Existing expert models estimate or marginalize source accuracy when scoring structures [1]; LLM-based approaches also use structural priors with a global confidence parameter [2]. We study repeated reports whose reliability can vary among variable pairs and whose error patterns can be shared across the source. We propose HB-NoisyKG , an observation model in the Dawid–Skene tradition [7, 28]. A DAG assigns each unordered pair a direct state: no edge or one of the two orientations. Reports provide noisy observations of that state. Pair reliability determines the probability of a correct report, and a shared error matrix describes the alternatives reported when the source errs. The model combines feature-conditioned pair reliability with a shared error distribution, linking report learning and graph inference through the pair-state marginals of a single DAG. Joint estimation uses report features

and agreement between reports and plausible graphs. This interface supports collapsed graph-only inference and joint inference over graphs and data-model parameters. We use “KG” as shorthand for the repeated causal reports. The experiments evaluate both structural recovery and source estimation. Budget-controlled com-parisons on five discrete benchmarks and a physical light-tunnel task establish gains over data-only inference. A controlled synthetic study tests the value of continued updating through reliability estimation error and prediction of independent held-out reports. Independent reliability models and post-fit pooling examine how the estimated trust levels and their assignment affect graph recovery. Accuracy, coverage, and elicitation studies identify conditions under which reports remain useful. Continuous Sachs [29] and generated ER graphs evaluate the interface across posterior families, and sparse binary experiments assess execution at 100 and 125 variables. We make the following contributions. 1. We formulate a likelihood for repeated causal reports with pair-specific reliability, a feature-conditioned prior, and a shared error matrix (Section 3.2). This makes report influence estimable within Bayesian graph inference. 2. We derive a report reward that depends only on the graph marginal, together with alternating graph, reliability, and confusion updates (Section 3.3). Closed-form or low-dimensional non-graph updates allow one observation-layer implementation to serve BDeu, BGe, and linear and nonlinear joint posteriors. 3. We demonstrate structural gains over data-only inference on five classical networks and a physical system. Controlled experiments show that continued updating reduces mean reliability estimation error and held-out report log loss compared with one-time estimation (Tables 1, 3, and 4). 

# 2 Related work 

Bayesian structure learning and knowledge guidance. Bayesian network (BN) structure learning spans score-based, constraint-based, MCMC, and differentiable approaches [16]; methods such as DiBS [22] move inference to continuous latent spaces and support both graph-only and joint graph-and-parameter inference. Prior knowledge has long been used through hard constraints, soft edge priors, and side constraints [13, 4, 8, 19, 25]. Amirkhani et al. [1] model forward, reverse, and absent-edge opinions, allow missing opinions, and estimate or marginalize expert-level accuracy when scoring structures. HB-NoisyKG instead assigns feature-conditioned reliability to repeatedly reported pairs and estimates a shared distribution over incorrect states alongside the graph posterior. VaMSL [3] extends DiBS to mixtures of causal graphs and incorporates actively elicited expert feedback through a graph prior. Our focus is the observation model for repeated reports of unknown reliability about a single graph. 

LLMs, noisy labels, and Dawid–Skene coupling. Recent work uses large language models as sources of causal hypotheses or structural priors [15, 35, 14, 6]. Harmonized-Prior [2] converts LLM output into a structural prior with globally tuned confidence c. MosaCD [23] combines conditional-independence tests with shuffled LLM queries to select reliable orientation seeds and prioritize propagation. Long et al. [21] use acyclicity and Markov-equivalence consistency to amend expert orientations. Vashishtha et al. [33] use causal order to address direct-versus-mediated ambiguity. HB-NoisyKG retains repeated reports in an observation likelihood with pair-specific reliability and a shared error matrix inferred alongside G. Its latent pair states are constrained by a common DAG, and the likelihood can be used with either collapsed or joint graph-posterior inference.

3.4 Instantiating the alternating inference procedure 

The graph backend supplies weighted graph samples for q(G). We use Stein variational gradient descent (SVGD) [20] on DiBS [22] for continuous joint inference, and Top-K local search for discrete benchmarks and graph-only Sachs. Their data models are BDeu [13], BGe [12, 18], and explicit linear or nonlinear SEM likelihoods. Before the first reliability update, we blend the data-only pair-state marginal with empirical report frequencies, weighted by report agreement. The blend helps avoid premature downweighting of consistent reports when the initial graph posterior is diffuse. The strength λwarm controls this one-time blend. It is 1 in the benchmark suite and is specified separately for the controlled studies. After each reliability update, a low-dimensional L-BFGS-B MAP step estimates (β, τ ) from Eq [log p(ρ |

β, τ )] + log p(β) + log p(τ ). Appendix B gives the update rules and study settings. Exact or objective-improving block updates make the variational/MAP objective nondecreasing; Appendix A states the corresponding conditions and the finite-report reliability bound.

# 4 Experiments 

We first evaluate structure recovery on the five discrete benchmarks and the physical light tunnel (Sections 4.2 and 4.3). We then study reliability estimation and continued updating (Section 4.4), followed by source quality, abstention, report semantics, and inference stability (Section 4.5). Contin-uous Sachs and generated ER graphs evaluate the interface across posterior families (Section 4.6). Section 4.7 reports computational cost.

25 edges), Insurance ( d = 27 , 52 edges), and Alarm ( d = 37 , 46 edges) from bnlearn. Their sample-size studies use N ∈ { 100 , 300 , 500 , 1000 , 5000 }. Continuous Sachs [29] has 11 variables and 7,466 observations, standardized by condition. The physical light-tunnel task from Causal Chambers [11] has 20 variables, ten disjoint blocks of 1,000 observations, and nested 250-row subsets. Reusing each dataset at three report accuracies gives 75 paired conditions per configuration. 

Reports. GPT-4.1-mini supplies repeated causal reports. The benchmark suite uses a causal-candidate prompt; the matched naming and reliability studies request direct parents. Four five-round batches per naming condition are paired with twenty data seeds. In the optical task, forty calls cover natural labels and random IDs with identical physical descriptions. Synthetic reports permit controlled changes in accuracy, edge retention, spurious claims, direction errors, and abstention. The naming intervention changes labels at a fixed domain and omits descriptions, measuring the role of variable semantics. The fixed channel also benefits from more search. Appendix E.8 gives the protocol, intervals, and secondary metrics. The matched direct-parent study in Table 2(a) tests report integration with a common Top-K backend and shared observations. After Holm correction across the five canonical-network SHD comparisons, the improvement over this control is significant only on Asia ( ∆SHD = −.91 , pHolm = .0026 ). 4.3 External reports in a physical system 

The light tunnel provides a physical task with an intervention-validated reference graph [11]. We use BGe as a linear-Gaussian approximation to its nonlinear measurements. GPT-4.1-mini receives the same physical descriptions under natural labels or random IDs. Four batches of five rounds per presentation give forty calls. All methods share the observational blocks, graph prior, and four-call search budget; the reference graph supplies the evaluation labels. The reports are sparse and imperfect: aggregated direct-edge F1 is .407 with natural labels and .395 

with IDs, covering 9.9% and 8.8% of unordered pairs. At N = 1000 with IDs, HB lowers mean SHD from 39 .00 to 27 .30 and raises AUROC from .738 to .764 (Table 3). The paired SHD difference is 

−11 .70 , with an exploratory 95% bootstrap interval [−16 .68 , −6.43] . HB also improves both means over no-KG at N = 250 . Report integration therefore improves recovery in this physical system under both variable presentations. HB also improves mean SHD and AUROC over independently learned common reliability in all four conditions. Fixed-reliability HB has higher AUROC throughout and lower SHD at N = 250 ; full HB has lower mean SHD at N = 1000 . The two criteria capture different uses of the inferred graph: selecting a structure and ranking possible edges. Appendix K reports E-SHD, Brier score, and the paired intervals.

4.5 Robustness under knowledge degradation 

We corrupt initially error-free synthetic reports on five networks, using 1,000 observations and three sampling seeds per setting. HB and no-KG share the observations and per-call search settings. Table 8 reports the clean condition and severity endpoints. HB retains lower mean SHD throughout the missing/spurious grid. Severe direction reversals and mixed errors exceed no-KG, illustrating the importance of report quality. At intermediate mixed corruption, SHD improves by 2.60 but AUROC falls to .848 . Mean reliability ranges from .42 to .55 across settings, averaging reported and unreported pairs. Figure 5 gives the full curves. A separate stability study extends inference to fifty outer updates over 5,508 settings with weak data and inaccurate reports. Every run remains finite, and two Top-K cases repeatedly alternate between two states. Tolerance attainment and recovery quality vary by backend and report accuracy (Appendix I). At accuracy .75 , heterogeneous pair-group errors combined with correlated reports give SHD re-ductions of 2.6 on Asia and 5.9 on Sachs. With 75% independent abstention, gains remain 2.6 and 

1.2. On Sachs at the same coverage, preferentially withholding incorrect reports preserves a 5.8

improvement; withholding correct reports increases SHD by 1.3. Source utility therefore depends on the quality of surviving reports as well as coverage. Complete abstention recovers no-KG exactly. For fully incorrect reports, increasing R from two to twenty reduces excess SHD from 5.0 to 1.8 on Asia and from 10 .0 to 1.2 on Sachs. Removing canonical names in the absence of variable descriptions sharply reduces report F1 (Ta-ble 2(b)). Names provide semantic information, and familiarity with public benchmarks acquired during pretraining may also improve report quality. 4.7 Computational cost 

Graph inference dominates runtime; the observation update costs O(d2R). Table 10 reports sparse binary experiments with 100 and 125 variables over ten paired seeds. HB lowers SHD by 47 .9

and 55 .4 at roughly three times no-KG runtime, using .202 and .254 GiB resident memory. The added cost reflects repeated graph updates, and the report layer remains usable beyond one hundred variables. 12 5 Conclusion 

HB-NoisyKG provides a common observation likelihood for repeated causal reports of unknown reliability. The same layer combines report evidence with discrete or continuous data and supports both graph-only and joint inference. Matched benchmark comparisons and the physical light-tunnel task demonstrate structural gains over data-only inference. The controls distinguish learning the level of trust from assigning fitted weights across pairs. In the controlled synthetic study, continued updating reduces reliability estimation error and improves held-out report prediction. Practitioners can use partial external reports alongside observations and estimate their influence during inference. The elicitation target should match the modeled relation, and coverage and source quality determine the information available. Completely incorrect reports remain challenging; absent reports recover data-only inference. The experiments give practical guidance on report count and initialization and demonstrate sparse recovery at 125 variables. Extending the observation interface to richer graph classes requires corresponding pair states and a suitable graph posterior, as illustrated by the controlled studies in Appendix J. 13 References

Dependent reports and heterogeneous errors. With the true pair state fixed, the Beta update counts matches and mismatches irrespective of the distribution among wrong labels. Heterogeneous off-diagonal errors affect joint inference through their influence on the graph posterior. Correlated reports provide less information: under a specified mixing model, a concentration inequality can use an effective sample size, while general dependence requires its own assumptions. Appendix E.6 examines heterogeneity and dependence separately and jointly. 

Conditional ascent of the inference objective. Write L(q, β, τ ) for the variational lower bound on the data and report likelihood, including log p(β) + log p(τ ) for the MAP empirical-Bayes step. Holding the other blocks fixed, exact maximization of a variational or parameter block cannot decrease L. The same statement holds for an inexact block update that is accepted only when it improves this objective. Applying this inequality successively to the graph, reliability, confusion, and empirical-Bayes blocks establishes conditional coordinate ascent. If the objective is bounded above, its values converge. Parameter convergence and graph recovery require additional conditions. Our Top-K and SVGD graph steps approximate the graph block. Their behavior is therefore assessed empirically in Appendix I. With weak observational evidence, repeated inaccurate reports can support an incorrect graph explanation even when the numerical updates remain well behaved. Approximate graph updates may also fail to improve the objective at every iteration. The audit distinguishes numerical failure, persistent oscillation, tolerance attainment, and statistical harm. Iteration limits and numerical or cycle monitoring provide practical diagnostics, with data-only inference available as a fallback.

# F Procedurally generated synthetic ER checks 

Prior structure of the Harmonized-Prior baseline. The Harmonized-Prior [2] (Algorithm 2, Eqs. 17–19) is a fixed-confidence path-existence soft prior: each LLM-asserted pair is read as a 

path-existence claim and directed reachability in G is rewarded via a shared confidence c. We sweep c ∈ { 0.60 , 0.70 , 0.80 , 0.90 , 0.99 , 0.99999 } as in their Figure 6 and report the best setting (c=0 .99999 ) in the main text and joint-panel rows. Even at its best sweep point, the Harmonized-Prior falls below the no-KG BGES-style baseline on the 11 LLM-asserted Sachs edges in the graph-only regime, and in the joint regimes it has nearly equal AUROC to DiBS on the linear SEM and lower AUROC on the nonlinear SEM. A wrong-direction pair claim can still be satisfied by a detouring chain of intermediate edges, and the reward then pulls those chains into G; the prior cannot adapt this tension on a per-pair basis.
