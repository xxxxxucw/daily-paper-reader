## SAMPLE DEPENDENCE-AWARE BLIND SOURCE SEPARATION FOR LINEAR CAUSAL DISCOVERY 

## Cihan Eralp Kumbasar, Emin Erdem Kumbasar, T¨ ulay Adali 

Dept. of CSEE, University of Maryland Baltimore County, Baltimore, MD 21250 

ABSTRACT 

Causal discovery seeks to determine directional relationships among variables in complex systems, yet observational data alone generally do not identify causal direction. Complete identification therefore requires assumptions beyond linearity and acyclicity. A key approach is the Linear Non-Gaussian Acyclic Model (LiNGAM), which formulates linear struc-tural equation model estimation as a blind source separation (BSS) problem. LiNGAM treats disturbances as mutually in-dependent random variables under independent sampling, re-lying on higher-order statistics (HOS) and non-Gaussianity for identifiability. This restricts identifiability when multiple disturbances are Gaussian and ignores potentially informative temporal dependence. To broaden causal identifiability, we formulate linear acyclic causal discovery with mutually inde-pendent disturbance processes and transfer established BSS identification conditions to causal structure recovery. Under this formulation, replacing the mutual information cost with mutual information rate enables identification under a broader class of disturbances, including Gaussian processes with non-proportional covariance functions. Synthetic experiments re-veal the limitations of methods relying solely on HOS or sam-ple dependence, while their joint exploitation provides near-perfect causal recovery across the tested disturbance settings. Experiments on real functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) data further demonstrate that jointly exploiting HOS and sample dependence improves the bootstrap stability of estimated ancestral directionality. 

Index Terms — Blind source separation, causal discov-ery, independent component analysis, mutual information rate, sample dependence 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Understanding how variables causally influence one another is important for explaining complex systems. Structural equa-tion models (SEMs) provide a principled framework for rep-resenting such relationships. However, in linear SEMs with

Gaussian disturbances, observational data provide informa-tion primarily through the covariance structure, which is gen-erally insufficient to uniquely identify the underlying directed causal graph [1, 2]. Consequently, such methods typically re-cover a Markov equivalence class of structures and require ad-ditional assumptions or prior information, such as the causal order, to uniquely determine the causal structure [3]. Linear Non-Gaussian Acyclic Model (LiNGAM) takes an alternative approach by exploiting non-Gaussianity to uniquely identify the linear causal structure from observational data, without requiring prior knowledge of the causal ordering [3, 4]. LiNGAM uses independent component analysis (ICA) [5] to solve the linear acyclic model and estimate the causal dependencies between variables. ICA provides a principled framework to blind source separation (BSS), with algorithms exploiting different forms of statistical diversity [5, 6]. Early source separation methods such as FastICA [7] commonly rely on higher-order statistics (HOS) and make use of the non-Gaussianity of the source distributions. Even though methods that only use non-Gaussianity cover the majority of ICA algorithms, source separation strategies are not limited to the adoption of HOS as the diversity among source signals. Alternative types of diversities, such as sample dependence, can also be used for source separation [6, 8, 9, 10, 11]. Classical LiNGAM models the disturbances as random variables sampled independently across observations, re-stricting the available BSS diversity to HOS and requiring that at most one disturbance is Gaussian for identifiability [3, 6]. However, many real-world signals exhibit sample de-pendence, whose temporal structure can provide additional statistical diversity for identification [6, 12]. Recent work has exploited this second-order temporal information for pair-wise instantaneous causal discovery [12]. A complementary direction is to jointly exploit HOS and sample dependence within a multivariate BSS formulation and transfer broader BSS identification conditions to causal structure recovery. In this work, we pursue this direction by extending the BSS interpretation underlying LiNGAM to mutually indepen-dent disturbance processes that may exhibit sample depen-dence. We transfer dependence-aware BSS identifiability re-sults to linear acyclic causal modeling and show that com-bining sample dependence with HOS enables causal struc-ture identification with Gaussian disturbance processes hav-arXiv:2610.06656v1 [eess.SP] 5 Oct 2026 ing distinguishable covariance functions. Synthetic experi-ments demonstrate the complementary roles of the two diver-sities: BSS methods relying solely on HOS fail with Gaus-sian disturbance processes, while methods relying only on sample dependence suffer when temporal diversity is insuf-ficient. Joint exploitation of both achieves near-perfect causal recovery across diverse disturbance settings. Beyond con-trolled simulations, we apply causal discovery to real func-tional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) data and assess the stability of the inferred group causal structure through bootstrap resampling of subjects. Joint use of HOS and sam-ple dependence yields more reproducible causal dependencies across variable pairs, while HOS-only methods show greater uncertainty in the directionality of ancestral relationships.

3. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS 3.1. Synthetic Simulations 

We compare four BSS algorithms for linear causal structure recovery. FastICA [7], the algorithm employed in original ICA-LiNGAM [3], and entropy bound minimization (EBM) [13] exploit HOS only, with EBM providing a more flexible source density model. Weights-adjusted second-order blind identification (WASOBI) only uses second-order statistics, and entropy rate bound minimization (ERBM) jointly exploits both diversities. We additionally include DirectLiNGAM since it also relies on non-Gaussianity despite not using ICA. We consider four statistical-diversity settings to com-pare the random-variable and random-process formulations. 

HOS-only uses generalized Gaussian distribution (GGD) dis-turbances with equal numbers of evenly spaced shape param-eters β in [0 .2, 0.8] and [1 .5, 8.0] , ensuring non-Gaussianity. 

Temporal-only uses Gaussian AR(1) disturbance processes, with AR coefficients chosen from evenly spaced grids over 

[−0.9, −0.1] ∪[0 .1, 0.9] to ensure temporal diversity. Temporal-dominant uses 20% GGD and 80% Gaussian AR(1) distur-bances, while HOS-dominant uses the opposite 80% /20% 

configuration. In each setting, we generate N = 20 variables with a ran-dom acyclic adjacency matrix B of density d = 0 .1, sampling edges from the N (N − 1) /2 permissible pairs. Nonzero coef-ficients are uniformly sampled from [−1.5, −0.5] ∪ [0 .5, 1.5] .We report the relative Frobenius error, || B − bB|| F /|| B|| F , to assess causal coefficient recovery, and AUPRC, computed as average precision, to assess edge detection. The simulation results are shown in Fig. 1. HOS-based methods perform well when non-Gaussianity is the dominant source of diversity, but degrade as Gaussian disturbances become dominant and fail to recover the true structure in the Temporal-only setting. Despite its more flexible density model, EBM performs similarly to FastICA in these settings, suggesting that density flexibility alone does not compensate for the omission of temporal information. WASOBI performs best in Temporal-only , where sample dependence is the only available diversity, but performs poorly in HOS-only 

and HOS-dominant , consistent with its reliance on temporal structure. Both mixed settings contain multiple Gaussian disturbances, which are not individually identifiable from marginal non-Gaussianity, and multiple disturbances with independent samples, which are indistinguishable through temporal covariance alone. Hence, neither source of di-versity alone guarantees complete causal recovery in these cases. Settings where only a single diversity is abundant also highlight a sample-efficiency tradeoff. In Temporal-only, WASOBI reaches an AUPRC close to one around T = 2000 ,whereas ERBM approaches comparable ranking performance near T = 10000 . Similarly, methods such as FastICA pro-vide better performance at low sample sizes in HOS-only .Thus, a specialized method is effective when its assumptions perfectly match the data, while joint exploitation performs more consistently across heterogeneous disturbance settings. 

3.2. Application to Real fMRI Data 

3.2.1. Dataset Details 

We analyze resting-state fMRI from K = 250 healthy con-trols in the BSNIP cohort [14, 15], with T = 224 time points per subject and TR = 2 s. Preprocessing included motion and slice-timing correction, Montreal Neurological Institute nor-malization, resampling, 6-mm smoothing, and brain masking. We employ threshold-free constrained independent vector analysis (tf-cIVA) [16], using the 53 NeuroMark templates as spatial references [17], to obtain aligned components across subjects. We restrict the analysis to N = 7 intrinsic connec-tivity networks (ICNs), comprising five subcortical (SC) and two auditory (AU) components. We standardize each com-ponent time course within each subject and concatenate the time courses across subjects along the temporal axis to form 

Xgroup ∈ RN ×KT . This places estimation in a more favor-able sample-to-dimension regime while enabling assessment of both within- and cross-domain ancestral stability.

4. CONCLUSION 

We introduced a random process formulation for BSS-based estimation of linear causal structures. The proposed frame-work enables the joint use of sample dependence and HOS, extending identifiability beyond the non-Gaussian distur-bance assumption of LiNGAM. Incorporating sample de-pendence enables causal structure identification with Gaus-sian disturbance processes when their covariance functions are sufficiently distinct. Synthetic experiments support the premise that jointly exploiting HOS and sample dependence improves causal structure recovery across diverse disturbance settings. HOS-based methods fail when non-Gaussianity is insufficient for identification, whereas methods relying solely on sample dependence perform poorly when the temporal diversity is limited. This contrast highlights the importance of jointly exploiting both forms of diversity. Experiments on real fMRI data further support this finding, with BSS meth-ods jointly exploiting HOS and sample dependence produc-ing more stable causal relationships across bootstrap samples compared to methods that rely only on HOS. These results demonstrate that exploiting statistical diversity beyond non-Gaussianity can broaden the identifiability of linear causal structure across a wider range of disturbance conditions. 5.
