ABSTRACT
Off-policy evaluation (OPE) is essential for assessing decision policies before de-
ployment when online experimentation is costly or unsafe. Accurate estimation
remains difficult when behavior-policy data are limited and the target policy favors
states and actions rarely observed in those data. We propose InCOPE, a prior-data
fitted network that learns a shared policy-value prediction rule across synthetic
OPE tasks. We construct an OPE task prior over Markov decision processes and
behavior–target policy pairs, generating behavior-policy datasets and exact target-
policy values for pretraining. For a new tabular decision problem, the pretrained
network uses the behavior-policy dataset, target policy, and known task settings
as context to predict the policy’s expected return without task-specific parame-
ter updates. Experiments across four tabular domains, including a semi-synthetic
clinical setting, demonstrate InCOPE’s effectiveness for policy-value estimation.

INTRODUCTION
Evaluating a decision policy before deployment is essential when online experimentation is costly
or unsafe. This concern arises in applications such as healthcare and intelligent tutoring (Gao et al.,
2023; 2024; Yu et al., 2024), online advertising (Xu et al., 2023), and ridesharing (Shi et al., 2024).
Off-policy evaluation (OPE) estimates a target policy’s expected cumulative return from trajectories
collected under a behavior policy, without deploying the target policy.
A substantial body of work has developed importance-sampling, model-based and value-based, dou-
bly robust, and marginalized-ratio estimators for OPE (Precup et al., 2000; Le et al., 2019; Jiang &
Li, 2016; Liu et al., 2018). With limited offline data, however, states and actions frequently visited
by the target policy may be poorly represented, particularly under substantial behavior–target policy
mismatch. Importance weighting can then suffer from high variance, while model-based and value-
based methods can produce inaccurate estimates for poorly observed state–action pairs (Le et al.,
2019; Liu et al., 2018). These difficulties motivate using knowledge acquired from related decision
problems to improve value estimation with limited current-task data.
Pretraining can encode cross-task knowledge in an estimator’s parameters. Prior-data fitted net-
works (PFNs) learn from synthetic datasets sampled from a task prior (M¨uller et al., 2022; Nagler,
2023), with applications to tabular prediction (Hollmann et al., 2025), causal inference (Ma et al.,
2026), and policy learning (Schiff et al., 2026; Zighem & Vie, 2026). For OPE, however, extending
PFNs to the evaluation of state-dependent target policies remains unexplored, to our knowledge.
The challenge is to infer a specified target policy’s cumulative return from finite behavior-policy
observations, accounting for how its actions and transition dynamics jointly determine that return.
To bridge this gap, we introduce InCOPE (In-Context Off-Policy Evaluation), a prior-data fitted
network for OPE. We learn to predict target-policy returns from behavior-policy observations by
pretraining on finite datasets paired with exact target-policy values, generated from Markov deci-
sion processes (MDPs) and policy pairs sampled from a task prior. The network encodes mean
rewards and visit counts for each state–action pair, then combines target-policy-weighted aggre-
gation with repeated propagation through estimated transitions. This incorporates successor-state
information under the queried policy, connecting observed local statistics to multi-step returns. At

We summarize our contributions as follows.
• We introduce InCOPE, a prior-data fitted network that learns a shared policy-value prediction rule
across synthetic OPE tasks. The pretrained network estimates target-policy values in new tasks
from historical data collected under behavior policies, without task-specific parameter updates.
• We formulate a joint prior over MDPs, behavior and target policies, and observed trajectories, and
characterize the Bayesian policy-value predictor targeted by prior fitting.
• Our empirical results across four structurally distinct tabular OPE task families show that InCOPE
improves policy-value estimation relative to representative OPE estimators when behavior-policy
data are limited and behavior and target policies differ substantially.

RELATED WORK
OPE estimators.
Existing OPE estimators use prediction, reweighting, or both.
Value-based
methods such as fitted Q evaluation learn target-policy value functions through Bellman regres-
sion (Le et al., 2019), whereas model-based methods evaluate the target policy using learned reward
and transition models (Gottesman et al., 2019). Importance sampling instead reweights observed
returns using behavior- and target-policy action probabilities (Precup et al., 2000). Marginalized
estimators and density-ratio methods replace products of importance ratios along trajectories with
state or state–action distribution ratios (Liu et al., 2018; Yin & Wang, 2020; Nachum et al., 2019;
Uehara et al., 2020). Doubly robust estimators bring prediction and reweighting together by com-
bining value estimates with importance-weighted residual corrections (Jiang & Li, 2016; Thomas &
Brunskill, 2016). Further refinements optimize regression-based corrections for statistical efficiency
(Farajtabar et al., 2018; Kallus & Uehara, 2019) or use reward shaping to construct control variates
for sparse rewards (Majumdar et al., 2026). At the estimator level, OPERA uses bootstrap estimates
of joint errors to combine multiple candidate estimators (Nie et al., 2024). These studies improve
estimation within individual OPE tasks, but do not address how to learn a reusable value-prediction
rule from related MDPs when data from a new task are scarce.
OPE with auxiliary information.
Beyond estimator design, structural priors and trajectory aug-
mentation can improve OPE with limited data. Structured Bayesian OPE uses an informative prior
to share reward information across correlated actions in contextual bandits (Aouali et al., 2025). For
sequential problems, OAT mines and augments sub-trajectories from behavior-policy data for down-
stream OPE (Gao et al., 2024). STITCH-OPE uses policy-guided diffusion to generate and stitch
target-policy trajectories, then evaluates policies using their predicted returns (Goli et al., 2025).
Auxiliary rollouts can also be combined with observed behavior-policy data, as in PERRY, which
uses correction or calibration to obtain value estimates and confidence intervals and permits pre-
trained generators (Mandyam et al., 2026). A complementary route encodes cross-task knowledge
directly in a pretrained value predictor without generating extra trajectories at inference time.
Prior-data fitted networks.
PFNs learn reusable predictors from synthetic tasks sampled from a
prior (M¨uller et al., 2022; Nagler, 2023). TabPFN applies this approach to tabular classification and
regression (Hollmann et al., 2023; 2025); LimiX-2 demonstrates synthetic pretraining’s potential for
tabular prediction (Zhang et al., 2026a). CausalPFN, Do-PFN, and CausalFM extend this approach
to causal inference (Balazadeh Meresht et al., 2025; Robertson et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2026). In
reinforcement learning, ICR-RL and PFN-RL use PFNs for policy learning (Schiff et al., 2026;
Zighem & Vie, 2026). For large language models, V0 uses a TabPFN head to predict a model’s
success probability on a new prompt from that model’s observed performance on previous prompts
(Zhang et al., 2026b). Sequential OPE poses a different prediction problem, requiring the expected
return of a specified target policy to be inferred from limited data collected under another policy
in a new MDP. InCOPE addresses this setting by pretraining a shared value predictor on synthetic
MDPs, with behavior-policy data as context and exact target-policy values as supervision.

In this section, we formulate the OPE problem and introduce the notation used throughout the paper.

H ≥1 is an integer horizon. We assume that the reward and next state are independent given (s, a).

INCOPE: IN-CONTEXT OFF-POLICY EVALUATION
InCOPE is pretrained on synthetic OPE tasks with known policy values. Given a target policy and a
new dataset collected under a behavior policy, it returns a value estimate in a forward pass without
parameter updates. This section describes task priors, network design, pretraining, and evaluation.

5.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
We evaluate InCOPE in four finite tabular domains. GridWorld models stochastic navigation with
grid-cell states, four directional actions, and varying layouts and reward mechanisms. Tumor models
treatment decisions in discretized tumor-growth systems, where chemotherapy and radiotherapy
affect tumor burden and incur treatment penalties. Dispatch represents a vehicle-service market,
with states describing the market zone and supply–demand imbalance, and actions combining driver
incentives and passenger promotions. The semi-synthetic Clinical evaluation uses the ICU-Sepsis
MDP (Choudhary et al., 2024), with aggregated patient states and 25 fluid–vasopressor treatment
combinations. Pretraining and evaluation use distinct environment generators within each domain.
Dynamics, rewards, and policy construction are detailed in Appendix C.1.
We evaluate two InCOPE configurations: domain-specific specialists pretrained on synthetic tasks
from their respective domains, and a unified model pretrained on pooled tasks from all four domains.
The unified model has the same architecture and parameter count as each specialist and uses no
domain identifier. Both configurations are evaluated in Sections 5.2 and 5.3; other experiments
use specialists. We train each configuration independently five times and select checkpoints using
source-validation data. Training details are given in Appendix B.3.
We compare with six OPE estimators. Weighted per-decision importance sampling (WPDIS) (Pre-
cup et al., 2000) reweights observed rewards using self-normalized importance weights. Fitted
Q evaluation (FQE) (Le et al., 2019) fits action-value functions by Bellman regression. Tabular
marginalized importance sampling (Tabular-MIS) (Yin & Wang, 2020) estimates target-policy state
occupancies and combines them with estimated rewards. Model-based OPE (MB-OPE) (Jiang &
Li, 2016) fits a pooled empirical MDP and evaluates the target policy by dynamic programming.
OPERA (Nie et al., 2024) combines candidate OPE estimators using data-dependent weights. Our
tabular implementation of PERRY’s DR-PPI (Mandyam et al., 2026) corrects model-based value es-
timates using held-out importance-sampling returns. See Appendix C.2 for implementation details.
In baseline comparisons, all methods share current-task data and target policies, with required
behavior-policy probabilities estimated from the data. InCOPE is pretrained with the loss in Eq. (4)
and frozen at test time. We measure mean absolute error (MAE) in original return units against exact
target-policy values from finite-horizon dynamic programming. Tables 2 and 3 report mean MAE ±
sample SD over five training replicates, with a separate shared test set per panel.
5.2

CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose InCOPE, a prior-data fitted network for off-policy evaluation in finite-
horizon tabular MDPs. By pretraining on synthetic OPE tasks with exact value labels, InCOPE
estimates target-policy values from data collected under a behavior policy without task-specific pa-
rameter updates. Experiments in four domains show more accurate OPE than the evaluated baselines
with limited data, while ablations support combining pretraining with current-task observations.

TRAINING DETAILS
Source data.
For the main comparison, each of the five training replicates uses 3,000 training
queries and 600 validation queries per family, generated before optimization and held fixed through-
out the run. Each query pairs an input context with its exact target-policy value; these counts refer to
queries rather than distinct MDPs. Source MDP groups are disjoint between training and validation.
Within each replicate, the specialist and unified models use the same source queries for each family.
Source generation, model initialization, and training-data order use separate random streams across
the five replicates.
Training objective and scales.
Training minimizes the objective in Eq. (8). For the main compar-
ison, MF = 3,000, with wF = 1 for the specialist’s single family and wF = 1/4 for each family in
the unified model. Writing eJF := medianj JF j, the default scale is
sF := 1.4826 medianj |JF j −eJF |.
If this value is nonfinite or below 10−6, the implementation tries the label interquartile range divided
by 1.349, the label population standard deviation, and max{| eJF |, 1} in that order, accepting the
first finite value at least 10−6. For a normal distribution, the median absolute deviation (MAD) and
interquartile range are approximately 0.67449 and 1.34898 times its standard deviation, respectively,
hence the multiplier 1.4826 and divisor 1.349. This calibration does not require normally distributed
policy-value labels. The threshold 10−6 and the final fallback are numerical safeguards against zero
or near-zero scales. The scales are computed separately for each replicate from training labels only
and remain fixed during optimization and validation. All family scales in the main comparison were
computed using the default MAD rule; no fallback was triggered.
Optimization and model selection.
Training uses AdamW with peak learning rate 3 × 10−4,
weight decay 10−3, and gradient-norm clipping at 2. Four microbatches of eight queries are accu-
mulated before each update, giving an effective batch size of 32. Each unified-model update contains
one microbatch from each family; a specialist draws all four from its own family. Queries are shuf-
fled within each family and reshuffled when the available queries are exhausted. An epoch contains
375 updates (12,000 draws) for the unified model and 94 updates (3,008 draws) for a specialist. The
learning rate uses one epoch of linear warmup followed by cosine decay toward 3 × 10−5 over a
maximum of 48 epochs.
After every epoch, validation averages the scaled squared errors over 600 queries per family, us-
ing the same family weights and training-derived scales as Eq. (8). Early stopping is enabled from
epoch 8, with patience 8: the patience counter resets only when validation loss decreases by more
than 0.003 relative to its last reset value. The checkpoint with the lowest validation loss is retained,
including improvements smaller than this threshold. These models are trained from random initial-
ization in PyTorch on Apple M4 hardware using the MPS backend.
