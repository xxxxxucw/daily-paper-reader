Abstract
Graph Foundation Models (GFMs) have recently emerged as a
promising paradigm for learning transferable representations across
different graphs and domains. However, existing GFMs face no-
table limitations, including limited pre-training scale or reliance
on aligning with textual information in Large Language Models
(LLMs). To overcome these limitations, recent studies have drawn
inspiration from prior-data fitted networks (PFNs), which learn
from large synthetic datasets and support multi-domain predic-
tion backed by causal learning. However, existing graph PFNs en-
counter severe difficulties in modeling causal relationships within
and across node and edge sets, generating diverse graph structures,
and designing suitable architectures that can learn from complex
synthetic datasets. To address these challenges, we propose GSCM-
PFN (Graph SCM-based Prior-Data Fitted Network), a graph PFN
pre-trained on synthetic data generated from an SCM-based prior
that jointly models nodes and graph topology. Specifically, we first
design a joint feature-edge-label SCM to guide the generation of
nodes and edges in synthetic graphs, enabling the PFN to cap-
ture causal relationships within and across node and edge sets.
Next, we introduce a prior-guided node-edge dependency model-
ing module that instantiates diverse synthetic graph generation

Introduction
Graph Foundation Models (GFMs) [15, 31, 51] have recently emerged
as a promising paradigm for learning transferable representations
across different graphs and domains. Unlike graph neural networks
(GNNs) [39, 43], which are often trained for specific tasks or do-
mains, GFMs are pre-trained on diverse graph data and can be
adapted to a broad range of downstream problems. This pre-training

then adapting paradigm offers a unified and flexible approach to
universal graph learning, with applications including molecular
property prediction [50] and knowledge graph reasoning [17]. On
the Web, GFMs can be applied to downstream tasks such as social
network analysis [52], recommender systems [19, 45], knowledge
graph mining [16], etc.
However, existing GFMs face notable limitations. For exam-
ple, GNN-based GFMs [15, 51] depend heavily on large-scale pre-
training data from diverse graph domains [31], yet such data may
be insufficient to support generalization across domains and tasks.
Meanwhile, LLM-based GFMs [8, 30] often rely on aligning with
textual information, which can lead to inconsistent data priors
when applied to graphs with few textual attributes [12, 13, 29].
To overcome these limitations, recent studies [12, 13, 24] have
drawn inspiration from the success of prior-data fitted networks
(PFNs) [22, 48], which achieve multi-domain prediction across di-
verse tabular datasets. PFNs, such as TabPFN [23] and LimiX-2 [49],
are pre-trained on diverse synthetic datasets to learn posterior pre-
dictive distributions induced by a structural causal model (SCM)
prior, thereby enabling effective in-context learning, particularly
for numerical attributes. One line of work [18, 29] has adapted PFNs
pre-trained in the tabular domain to graph foundation models, as in
G2T-FM [12] and TAG [20]. Although these models have achieved
reasonable performance, they still rely heavily on hand-crafted
features and lack large-scale pre-training on diverse graph data,
limiting their ability to capture complex graph patterns [13].
Another line of work [9, 13] develops graph-specific priors for
multi-domain node classification that natively incorporate topology,
as in GraphPFN [13] and NodePFN [9]. However, they face three
unresolved major challenges. First, although both models use SCM
priors to generate node features, they rely on the Erdős–Rényi (ER)
model and methods in the stochastic block model (SBM) family to
generate graph topology [2, 11, 21, 25], rather than on a unified SCM
that jointly models causal dependencies within node and edge sets
and between them. Such an SCM could provide a more principled
basis for generalization, since causal dependencies both exist within
node and edge sets and between them [4, 5, 28, 40]. For example,
node attributes can affect other nodes and give rise to edges, while
existing edges can alter node attributes and lead to further edges.
Second, generating diverse synthetic graph data is more challeng-
ing than generating tabular data, since graph topologies can exhibit
complex dependencies among edges. The existing methods rely on
ER-based and SBM-family priors, which do not explicitly capture
several such dependencies, including triadic closure [44], degree
assortativity [35], and continuous core-periphery structure [3]. The
synthetic graphs therefore cover a narrower range of topologies.
Lastly, designing an architecture that can learn from such com-
plex synthetic datasets is also a challenge. These datasets combine
heterogeneous feature semantics which differ across graphs with
topological dependencies that vary widely in range. NodePFN does
not guarantee feature-permutation invariance, so it fails to model
inputs whose feature dimensions do not share a fixed meaning.
GraphPFN augments the tabular LimiX model by stacking message-
passing layers in series, but this design cannot capture complex
and diverse graph structures.
To address these challenges, we propose GSCM-PFN (Graph
SCM-based Prior-Data Fitted Network), a PFN-based multi-domain
graph foundation model. Specifically, GSCM-PFN is pre-trained
on synthetic data generated from an SCM-based prior that jointly
models nodes and graph topology through three tailored modules
to address the challenges outlined above. First, we design a joint
feature-edge-label SCM to coordinate the generation of nodes and
edges in synthetic graphs. Through a graph causal-relation decou-
pling mechanism, the SCM lets the model capture causal relation-
ships within the node set and the edge set, as well as between them.
Next, we introduce a prior-guided node-edge dependency model-
ing module that carries out the synthetic graph generation process
specified by the SCM. This module yields diverse generation strate-
gies by composing multiple layers, which exchange information
between nodes and edges at the sample level. We prove that this
strategy is strictly more expressive than the ER and SBM priors
used in existing methods. Finally, we propose a feature-permutation-
invariant dual-path graph network for learning on synthetic graphs
sampled from complex SCM priors. The network combines a prov-
ably feature-permutation-invariant design with graph transformer
layers, which makes it suitable for learning from diverse synthetic
graph data with heterogeneous feature semantics. We evaluate
our method on real-world benchmarks and the results show that
GSCM-PFN outperforms both conventional GFMs and PFN-based
GFMs, without using real-world graph data for pre-training.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose GSCM-PFN, a PFN-based multi-domain graph
foundation model pre-trained on causally inspired synthetic
graphs instead of real-world graph data.
• We develop a joint feature-edge-label SCM to guide the prior-
guided node-edge dependency modeling module for diverse
graph generation, modeling causal dependencies within and
between the node and edge sets. We prove that our strategy is
more expressive than existing ER and Bernoulli SBM priors.
• We design a feature-permutation-invariant dual-path graph
network that handles heterogeneous feature semantics and
captures graph topology with theoretical guarantees.
• GSCM-PFN outperforms both conventional GFMs and PFN-
based GFMs on 23 real-world benchmarks, while only utilizing
synthetic data during pre-training.

Preliminaries and Problem Formulation
Given a graph G = (V, A, X, ytrain), where V is the node set,
A ∈{0, 1}|V|×|V| is the adjacency matrix, X ∈R|V|×𝑑is the
node feature matrix with feature dimension 𝑑, and ytrain is a node-
indexed vector of observed labels whose observed entries identify
the labeled nodes Vtrain ⊂V, the goal of node classification is to
predict the test-label vector ytest, whose entries are the labels of the
remaining nodes Vtest = V \ Vtrain.
Following [34], we view the unknown distribution 𝑝0 of a fully
labeled graph as one draw from a prior Π. Sampling from this prior
proceeds by drawing 𝑝∼Π and then drawing the fully labeled
graph from 𝑝. A subset of these labels is observed and collected
in ytrain. The remaining labels form ytest. The posterior predictive
distribution (PPD) is the conditional distribution of ytest given G
under this sampling scheme.

Method
In this section, we introduce GSCM-PFN and its three components,
as shown in Figure 1. First, the joint feature-edge-label SCM de-
fines a joint causal prior over node features, graph topology, and
labels. Next, the prior-guided node-edge dependency modeling mod-
ule instantiates diverse graph generation strategies through mul-
tilayer causal updates of features, labels, and edges. Finally, the
feature-permutation-invariant dual-path graph network supports
in-context node classification.
3.1
Joint Feature-Edge-Label SCM
The module is designed to guide the joint generation of nodes and
edges in synthetic graphs. Unlike the sequential graph generation
strategies used in NodePFN [9] and GraphPFN [13], which generate
node features before edges or vice versa, our approach uses a graph
causal mechanism to generate nodes and edges simultaneously.
Our method first defines graph-level latent variables for the gen-
eration mechanism. Specifically, we use a two-stage decoupling
strategy to decompose the abstract graph latent variable into com-
ponents that can be instantiated by the generation algorithm. First,
considering node and edge information have distinct semantics,
we separate the node-related latent variables from the edge-related
latent variables and represent the edge-related variables as a vector
of scalar components 𝑍𝐴= (𝑍𝐴,1, . . . ,𝑍𝐴,𝑑𝐴), where 𝑑𝐴denotes
the number of scalar components in this group. Second, coupling
node features with labels can weaken the causal relations between
labels and other graph elements, particularly in a complex graph-
generating SCM. Tabular PFNs primarily model causal dependen-
cies between features and labels under an i.i.d. sample assumption.
Graph generation, by contrast, must also capture dependencies
within and across node and edge sets, resulting in a more complex
SCM with potentially multi-hop causal pathways. This complexity
makes it difficult to preserve the causal relationships between labels
and other graph elements, particularly when label variables are
coupled with a much larger set of node features. We therefore fur-
ther decompose the node-related latent variables into node-feature
latent variables 𝑍𝑋= (𝑍𝑋,1, . . . ,𝑍𝑋,𝑑𝑋) and node-label latent vari-

of scalar components in their respective groups.

3.3
Feature-Permutation-Invariant Dual-Path
Graph Network
Lastly, we design a backbone architecture to learn from the syn-
thetic graphs while addressing two challenges. First, feature seman-
tics are heterogeneous across both synthetic and real-world graphs,
and the meaning of a given feature dimension may differ from one
graph to another. These heterogeneous feature semantics limit the
direct use of standard graph architectures such as GNNs [39, 43]
and graph Transformers [6, 38]. They rely on order-sensitive inter-
feature information exchange, implicitly assuming that a given
feature dimension has a consistent meaning across graphs [27].
This assumption keeps them from being feature-permutation in-
variant, thereby making them ill-equipped to generalize across
arbitrary graphs with varying features [15]. Second, graphs vary
in the range and strength of their topological dependencies. Some
exhibit long-range dependencies that benefit from deeper message
passing, whereas others involve weak neighbor influence, making
extensive message passing unnecessary.

feature semantics across graphs with encoders for node features
and labels, followed by feature-level and sample-level attention.

4.1
Experimental Setup
Datasets. Following [9], we evaluate our method on 23 bench-
mark datasets spanning a wide range of domains, including aca-
demic collaboration, social, e-commerce, and airline route networks.
The task is node classification and the datasets contain between 2
and 15 classes. Further details are provided in Appendix C.1.
Baselines. We compare GSCM-PFN against baselines from three
groups. The first comprises standard GNNs, including GCN [26] and
GAT [43], trained from scratch on each dataset. The second com-
prises conventional GFMs, including GraphAny [51], TS-GNN [15],
and RGVT [27]. The third comprises PFN-based GFMs, including
GraphPFN [13] and NodePFN [9]. To ensure a fair comparison, we
adapt both GFM groups using only the training split of each target
dataset, with hyperparameters fixed across datasets.
Implementation Details. We realize GSCM-PFN as a 9-layer
transformer with embedding dimension 96, three attention heads
of dimension 32, and four position-wise feed forward networks of
hidden size 384 with GELU activations in each layer. Every sublayer
uses pre-normalization with a LayerNorm that has no affine param-
eters. A linear head maps the test-node label representations to 20
class logits. Optimization uses AdamW with learning rate 3 × 10−4,
no weight decay, gradient clipping at 1, and a cosine schedule that
warms up for 5 epochs and decays to 10−5. Mixed precision is en-
abled. We train for 50 epochs on 128 GPUs, with one graph per
GPU and 1000 steps per epoch, and use the epoch-50 checkpoint.
Only the training split is provided as labeled context; validation
and test nodes are queries, and their labels are masked. Edges are
symmetrized and self-loops are removed. We follow the fixed split
of each dataset, repeat the evaluation for five runs, and report mean
accuracy and standard deviation. Additional implementation details
are provided in Appendix C.2.

Ablation Studies. To answer RQ3, we ablate six components
of the synthetic prior and two components of the model archi-
tecture. For the synthetic prior, w/o prior-guided message passing
removes the prior-guided node-edge dependency modeling mod-
ule. w/o 𝑌Decouple removes the separate latent group for node
labels, and w/o 𝐴Decouple removes the separate latent group for
edges. w/o multilayer structure replaces the multiple prior-guided
node-edge dependency modeling layers with a single layer. w/o
feature relationships removes the linear transformations that model
causal dependencies among variables within each group. w/o sample
relationships removes the joint layerwise updates of the feature-
, label-, and edge-related variables, which capture sample-level
dependencies. For the architecture, w/o feature cross-reduction re-
places feature cross-reduction with the mean of the attention and
message-passing pathways. w/o message passing removes feature-
wise message passing along graph edges.
Table 2 reports the average test accuracy, with drops measured
relative to the full model. The prior-guided node-edge dependency
modeling module is the most important prior component for gener-
ating diverse synthetic graphs with structured dependencies, and
removing it causes the largest drop. Explicitly modeling label- and
edge-related latent variables benefits the prior, as shown by w/o
𝑌Decouple and w/o 𝐴Decouple. Multilayer causal updates help
capture dependencies across hops, and w/o multilayer structure low-
ers accuracy. Feature-level relationships improve the prior, while
sample-level dependencies contribute more, with w/o sample rela-
tionships dropping further than w/o feature relationships. On the
architecture, message passing makes a substantial contribution
to capturing graph topology, and feature cross-reduction adds a
smaller but consistent benefit by combining the two pathways.
Overall, each component of the synthetic prior and the model ar-
chitecture contributes to GSCM-PFN’s performance, with the prior-
guided node-edge dependency modeling module and the message-
passing pathway showing the largest effects.

Related Work
5.1
Graph Foundation Models
GFMs aim to transfer knowledge across graph datasets and gener-
alize to previously unseen graphs and tasks. From an architectural
perspective, they can be broadly grouped into GNN-based [15, 51],
LLM-based [8], and hybrid GNN+LLM models [30].
GNN-based models. They exploit graph structure through
topology-aware inductive biases. For example, GraphAny [51] for-
mulates inference on a new graph as an analytical LinearGNN
solution and combines predictions from multiple such solutions us-
ing inductive attention. SAMGPT [47] pre-trains a message-passing
encoder on text-free graphs from multiple domains, inserting learn-
able structure tokens into each layer to align topology-dependent
aggregation, and adapts the frozen encoder to node classification
on an unseen graph with dual structural prompts.
LLM-based models. They incorporate semantic information
through natural language processing. InstructGLM [46], for in-
stance, extends the vocabulary of a pre-trained language model by
encoding node features as distinct tokens, facilitating cross-modal
graph understanding. LLaGA [7] reorganizes the neighborhood of
a target node into a structure-aware sequence and maps it into the
token embedding space of a frozen language model, which then
performs node classification on citation and product graphs.
Hybrid GNN+LLM models. They integrate structural and tex-
tual information. For example, OFA [30] aligns textual descriptions
of nodes and edges into a shared embedding space through language
modeling. GraphGPT [41] pairs this with the opposite interface:
a graph encoder produces structural embeddings, a lightweight
projector turns them into graph tokens, and two-stage instruction
tuning teaches the language model to classify nodes in both super-
vised and zero-shot settings.
Despite these advances, existing approaches remain constrained
by their underlying architectures, including limited pre-training
scale [31] and reliance on textual alignment [12, 13].
5.2
Prior-data Fitted Networks
PFNs offer an alternative to conventional pre-training by learn-
ing in-context prediction from synthetic tasks sampled from prior
distributions over data-generating processes. TabPFN [22, 23] and
LimiX [48, 49] have demonstrated strong performance on tabular
tasks, while other works, such as TuneTables [14] and BETA [32],
have extended PFN approaches to larger and real-world datasets.
Recent work has brought this paradigm to graph tasks [12, 13, 33]
along two broad directions. The first direction adapts tabular PFNs
to graph prediction. For example, G2T-FM [12] and TAG [20] apply
tabular PFN approaches to graph tasks. However, these methods rely
heavily on hand-crafted features and lack large-scale pre-training
on diverse graph data, which limits their ability to capture complex
graph patterns [13]. The second direction develops graph-specific
priors for multi-domain node classification, as in GraphPFN [13]
and NodePFN [9]. Despite these advances, existing methods still en-
counter severe difficulties in jointly modeling causal relationships
within and across node and edge sets, generating diverse graph
structures, and designing suitable architectures that can learn from
complex synthetic datasets.

Conclusion
In this work, we propose GSCM-PFN, a graph PFN pre-trained
on synthetic data generated from an SCM-based prior that jointly
models nodes and graph topology. Our method effectively achieves
this goal by integrating three key components, including the joint
feature-edge-label SCM, the prior-guided node-edge dependency
modeling module, and the feature-permutation-invariant dual-path
graph network. Extensive experiments on 23 real-world bench-
marks demonstrate that GSCM-PFN outperforms both conven-
tional GFMs and PFN-based GFMs, without utilizing real-world
graph data for pre-training.

This study uses benchmark graph datasets and synthetically gener-
ated data and does not involve human participants or the collection
of new personal information. We have not identified any specific
ethical or privacy concerns arising from this work.
