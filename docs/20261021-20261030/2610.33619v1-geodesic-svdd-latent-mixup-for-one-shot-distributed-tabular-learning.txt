Abstract —Distributed Learning on structured data is a key and widely adopted approach in privacy-sensitive sectors, ranging from financial fraud prevention to federated healthcare diagnos-tics. While Gradient Boosted Decision Trees are widely used in centralized settings due to their efficiency, they are incompatible with standard gradient-based federated learning protocols be-cause of their non-differentiability. Conversely, standard deep classifiers that are amenable to federated optimization often suffer from catastrophic forgetting under label distribution skew. In this work, to address these gaps we propose Geodesic-SVDD, a framework for Distributed Spherical Prototype Learning that recasts each client’s task as one-class density estimation on the hypersphere, so that local models can be aggregated as probabilistic manifolds in a single round. Specifically, for each client, we employ a Residual Spherical Encoder and Geodesic Latent Mixup that enforces strict hyperspherical constraints (∥ϕ(x)∥ ≡ 1), preventing the manifold collapse observed in linear approximations. Furthermore, leveraging this insight, we propose a novel One-Shot “Product of Experts” protocol that enables privacy-preserving aggregation without iterative syn-chronization. Finally, we mechanistically analyze the trade-off between interpolation strategies, demonstrating that while linear mixup offers an enticing computational shortcut, it destabilizes optimization gradients by orders of magnitude ( 10 9×). Across five tabular benchmarks and four levels of label skew, Geodesic-SVDD attains the highest mean AUROC among seven federated methods, outperforms federated tree ensembles by up to 21 AUROC points on Covertype , and its One-Shot variant matches centralized Deep SVDD under IID and mild skew with a single communication round. 

Index Terms —Tabular Data, Deep SVDD, Federated Learning, Geodesic Mixup, Non-IID Data, Spherical Manifold. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Distributed Learning, often referred to as Federated Learn-ing (FL) [1] on complex structured data (e.g., tabular) is a key requirement for safety and privacy sensitive sectors, ranging from classification tasks in financial fraud prevention to feder-ated healthcare diagnostics. In centralized environments, Gra-dient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) and Isolation Forests are the de facto standard due to their robustness on irregular manifolds [2]. However, deploying these models in distributed classification presents what we term the “ Federated Tabular Gap .” Tree ensembles are inherently non-differentiable and difficult to aggregate privacy-preservingly without sharing raw data splits or generating synthetic proxies [3]. This limits their accuracy on complex benchmarks such as Covertype ,as our experiments show (Sec. IV). Deep Learning offers a

differentiable alternative via weights aggregation algorithms like FedAvg [1]. However, standard deep classifiers are no-toriously brittle under Label Distribution Skew , where clients possess disjoint subsets of the global classes [4]. Averaging the weights of neural networks trained on disjoint manifolds results in destructive interference and leads to poor global generalization performance [5], which renders the global model unreliable for critical decision-making. Furthermore, these methods typically assume fully labeled datasets, which are rarely available in real edge deployment. To bridge this gap, we propose Geodesic-SVDD , a frame-work for Distributed Spherical Prototype Learning. Unlike standard classifiers that partition space with hyperplanes, our approach treats local learning as an unsupervised density estimation problem [6]. We map inputs to a compact prototype on a hyperspherical manifold ( Sd−1), allowing clients to learn valid feature representations without requiring contrasting class labels. We then aggregate these local experts via a 

“Product of Experts” (PoE) protocol [7]. Crucially, this architecture eases the traditional trade-off between communication efficiency [8] and model robustness. We demonstrate that by aggregating unsupervised probabilistic manifolds rather than supervised weights, we can approach the performance of centralized baselines using a single-round One-Shot protocol. This capability significantly reduces the communication burden in non-IID settings, limiting the expo-sure to iterative gradient leakage [9] while matching a model trained on pooled data under IID and mild skew. A critical design choice for algorithmic stability is the enforcement of geometric integrity during local training. To handle data sparsity, we employ Geodesic Latent Mixup . While our em-pirical analysis reveals that standard Linear Interpolation [10] can achieve competitive predictive scores, we demonstrate that such approximations induce Manifold Collapse (midpoint norm 0.71 for random pairs) and severe gradient instabil-ity ( 10 9× gradient variance). By strictly interpolating along the Riemannian manifold via Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) [11], Geodesic-SVDD ensures that high detection performance is grounded in valid boundary definitions, aprerequisite for reliable deployment. We identify three challenges to deploying robust, privacy-preserving models in non-IID distributed tabular environments: 

1) The Tabular Task : In centralized settings, Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDTs) dominate tabular learning due to their robustness on irregular manifolds [2], [12]. How-ever, their non-differentiability creates a “ Federated Tabular Gap ,” obstructing gradient aggregation without costly cryptog-raphy or synthetic proxies [13]. As shown in Sec. IV, federated tree ensembles plateau at 0.71 –0.76 AUROC on Covertype ,where our method reaches 0.91 . A differentiable framework recovering tree-like robustness is therefore required. 

2) Non-IID Scenarios : Real-world federated data fre-quently exhibits Label Distribution Skew . In extreme cases, clients observe disjoint class subsets, causing standard 

FedAvg to suffer from weight divergence that grows with the degree of skew [14], [15]. To address this, we recast local learning as single-prototype density estimation, in the spirit of 

prototype aggregation [16]. 

3) The Constraints of Iterative Communication : Standard iterative FL requires persistent bi-directional channels, widen-ing the attack surface for deep leakage reconstruction [9]. While “One-Shot” protocols minimize this exposure [17], ag-gregating disjoint experts without iterative alignment remains a significant open challenge.

2) Geodesic vs. Linear Interpolation : To robustly define decision boundaries on this manifold under data sparsity, we employ latent mixup. As depicted in Fig. 1 (Top-Right), standard linear interpolation (LERP [10], [18]) takes the chordal path ˜zlin = λz i + (1 − λ)zj . This operation violates the manifold geometry, as ∥˜zlin ∥ < 1 for any λ ∈ (0 , 1) ,collapsing the synthetic sample into the interior of the sphere. To preserve geometric integrity, we employ Spherical Lin-ear Interpolation (SLERP [11]), which traverses the geodesic arc:

For a dataset with classes C, we conduct |C| distinct experiments. In each experiment, a single class c ∈ C is designated as the normal 

class, and the models are trained exclusively on samples where y = c (one-class setting). During evaluation, samples from class c are labeled negative (0), while samples from all other classes ( y̸ = c) are labeled positive (anomalous, 1). We report the macro-average of AUROC , Accuracy, Pre-cision, Recall, and F1 across these experiments. Threshold-dependent metrics are computed at the optimal operating point determined via Youden’s J statistic [27] on a held-out server-side validation set containing both in-distribution and out-of-distribution samples. Results are averaged over 5 independent seeded runs (mean ± std). Unlike supervised baselines that leverage contrasting labels to learn discriminative boundaries, our method operates as a One-Class Classifier . We solve the strictly harder problem of density estimation without access to negative samples during local training.

D. Efficiency vs. Accuracy Trade-off 

While predictive performance is paramount, one of the primary motivations for the One-Shot protocol is communica-tion efficiency in bandwidth-constrained settings. We quantify the communication cost relative to the model size M (in MB). Standard FedAvg and Iterative Geodesic-SVDD require 

2 × R transmissions (upload/download) per client, resulting in a total communication cost of Citer = 2 · R · K · M .In contrast, our One-Shot protocol requires a single upload, reducing cost to COS = K ·M . We exclude the warm-up phase communication overhead from this analysis, as transmitting a single d-dimensional latent statistics vector ( E[ϕ(x)] ) is asymptotically negligible compared to the full model size M .For a standard training session of R = 50 rounds, Geodesic-SVDD (OS) reduces communication overhead by a factor of 

100 ×. As seen in Table I, on the Breast Cancer dataset, this 

100 × reduction in bandwidth costs no AUROC under IID (0.992 vs. 0.991 for the iterative protocol) and at most 1.2

points under extreme skew ( 0.978 vs. 0.990 ). However, on complex manifolds like Covertype (Fig. 3), iterative alignment is necessary at high skew: at α = 0 .01 , it recovers 15 of the 16 AUROC points lost by the One-Shot protocol ( 0.76 → 0.91 ).

In this work, we addressed the fundamental incompatibility between state-of-the-art tabular learning and Federated Learn-ing—the “Federated Tabular Gap.” We introduced Geodesic-SVDD , a framework for Distributed Spherical Prototype Learning that recasts federated tabular learning as one-class density estimation on the hypersphere. Our results indicate that this recasting is what neutralizes label skew: under a single-center objective, iterative weight averaging remains stable even when clients hold nearly disjoint classes, with Geodesic-SVDD (Iter) staying within 1.5 AUROC points of its IID performance on every benchmark. By mapping each client’s data to a compact prototype on a hyperspherical manifold (Sd−1), the same formulation enables a Product of Experts (PoE) aggregation protocol that requires a single upload per client. In the One-Shot setting, this additive density estimation lets the global model form a “Union of Manifolds” from a single upload per client, matching centralized Deep SVDD under IID and mild skew. With iterative alignment, Geodesic-SVDD attains the highest mean AUROC among the seven federated methods we evaluate and outperforms federated tree ensembles by up to 21 AUROC points on Covertype .Our results suggest a clear decision rule for practitioners based on the “Privacy-Utility-Skew” triangle: High-Security / Low-Bandwidth (One-Shot): In scenarios where iterative communication is prohibitive (e.g., cross-silo financial fraud detection), our One-Shot protocol offers a highly efficient solution. By uploading a single, valid probabilistic manifold, clients match centralized Deep SVDD on IID and mild-skew data without ever exposing gradient updates. Extreme Hetero-geneity (Iterative): In “worst-case” regimes where clients hold mutually exclusive classes (Extreme Skew, α = 0 .01 ), the iterative alignment of these manifolds becomes necessary, re-covering the One-Shot losses (e.g., from 0.76 to 0.91 AUROC on Covertype ). While our focus is on the architectural framework, our abla-tion study highlights a critical implementation detail. Although linear approximations (Linear-SVDD) can empirically match the predictive performance of rigorous geodesic interpolation, they do so at the cost of numerical stability ( 10 9× gradient variance). For safety-critical systems, strictly enforcing the Riemannian geometry via SLERP is required to guarantee reliable convergence, ensuring that the theoretical “normality” constraints of the SVDD objective are never violated.
