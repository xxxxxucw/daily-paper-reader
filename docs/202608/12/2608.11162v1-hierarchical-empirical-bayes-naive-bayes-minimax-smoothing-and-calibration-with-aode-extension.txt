Abstract 

The Naive Bayes (NB) classifier remains a standard choice for categorical data, yet its widely used smoothing rules, such as Laplace, Lidstone, Krichevsky–Trofimov, and the m-estimate, all prescribe a fixed smoothing strength that ignores feature cardi-nality, sample size, and class imbalance, inducing a non-vanishing bias on modern high-cardinality tabular data. We propose hierarchical empirical-Bayes Naive Bayes (HEB-NB), in which each class-feature conditional probability is smoothed by a Diri-chlet prior whose concentration is learned data-adaptively via Type-II maximum likelihood, enabling principled information sharing across classes while retaining closed-form inference. We further introduce HEB average one-dependence estimators (HEB-AODE), showing that the adaptive smoothing transfers cleanly to structural relaxations of NB. Theoretically, we establish a non-asymptotic 

ℓ1 error bound for HEB-NB matching the empirical-distribution minimax rate plus a vanishing data-adaptive bias, together with a matching Laplace-tight lower bound that yields a finite-sample, risk-level strict separation from Laplace. We further derive a plug-in excess Bayes-risk bound via total-variation tensorization and a population top-1 expected calibration error (ECE) corollary. Empirically, across 31 UCI and OpenML benchmarks, HEB-NB attains the best average Friedman rank on probabilistic metrics, with up to 22.1% log-loss reductions on high-cardinality datasets and consistent improvements of HEB-AODE over vanilla AODE. Combining HEB-NB with mutual-information weighting reduces top-1 ECE by 41%–70%, demonstrating substantial gains in probabilistic accuracy and calibration.

1. Introduction 

The Naive Bayes (NB) classifier approximates the joint dis-tribution of a label Y and a feature vector X = (X1, . . . , XF )through the conditional-independence factorization P(X | Y) =

QFf =1 P(X f | Y). Despite the well-known inadequacy of this as-sumption (Hand and Yu, 2001; Zhang, 2004), NB remains a workhorse for categorical and binned tabular data due to its robustness in small-sample regimes and its transparent deci-sion rule. The recent NB literature has refined the classifier along two axes: (i) attribute and instance weighting, which rescales each log ˆ P(X f | Y) in the posterior (He et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2021; Zhang and Jiang, 2022; Zhou et al., 2022); and ( ii ) structural relaxation, which adds limited dependencies among features, as in tree augmented NB (Friedman et al., 1997), average one-dependence estima-tors (AODE) (Webb et al., 2005), and hidden NB (Jiang et al., 2009). Both refinement axes treat the conditional probability estima-tor ˆ P(X f | Y) as a fixed input. The estimator itself has received

Every realistic NB implementation applies smoothing such that empirical counts are augmented with non-negative pseudo-counts before normal-ization, both to avoid zero-probability pathologies and to sta-bilize rare-category estimates. The main contribu-tions of this work are summarized as follows: 

1) HEB-NB Estimator (Section 3.1) : A closed-form, hyperparameter-free smoothed categorical NB classifier whose per-(class, feature) Dirichlet concentration is learned by Type-II ML (Algorithm 1), with two natural prior-mean variants HEB-U and HEB-M. Limit cases recover Laplace smoothing and the full-shrinkage endpoint of Micci-Barreca TE, so HEB-NB uni-fies both within a single hierarchical-Bayes family while pre-serving the time and memory complexity of categorical NB up to a constant factor. 

2) HEB-AODE Structural Extension (Section 3.2) : A hierar-chical Type-II ML smoother for AODE’s pairwise conditionals, in which the concentration is shared across the super-parent’s instantiations and learned by a multi-document extension of Minka’s single-document fixed-point iteration. HEB-AODE re-covers vanilla AODE as a limit case and demonstrates that the HEB smoothing improvement transfers cleanly across the struc-tural axis of NB refinement. 

3) Non-Asymptotic Theoretical Analysis (Section 4) : We es-tablish ( i) a pathwise ℓ1 upper bound for HEB-NB (Theorem 1) that matches the empirical-distribution rate of Han et al. (2015, Theorem 1) plus a data-adaptive bias, in contrast to Laplace’s deterministic non-vanishing bias; ( ii ) a rigorous sanity bound on the HEB shrinkage factor (Lemma 1) via the determinis-tic clamp from Assumption 3, ensuring that the bias vanishes asymptotically as the within-class sample size grows; ( iii ) a matching lower bound for Laplace (Theorem 2) proving its bias is Laplace-tight at the simplex vertex; ( iv ) a rigorous, finite-sample, risk-level strict separation between HEB-NB and Laplace (Corollary 1) that cannot be attributed to bound loose-ness in either estimator; and ( v) an excess Bayes-risk bound via joint total-variation (TV) tensorization (Corollary 2), which does not require any uniform lower bound on P(X), a property essential in the high-cardinality regime, together with a cor-responding population top-1 expected calibration error (ECE) bound (Theorem 3). 

4) Comprehensive Empirical Study (Section 5) : Across 31 UCI and OpenML datasets (including three high-cardinality benchmarks with up to 16 ,137 categories per feature), HEB-M attains the best mean Friedman rank on log-loss and Brier, and significantly dominates 13 of 24 baseline–metric pairs un-der the Nemenyi protocol at α = 0.05. HEB-AODE signifi-cantly improves over vanilla AODE on F1, log-loss, and Brier (paired Wilcoxon p ≤ 0.029 on each), with log-loss reductions of up to 22.1% on click-prediction. Combining HEB-M with sqrt-mutual-information (sqrt-MI) weighting cuts top-1 ECE by 41%–70% versus Laplace on the high-cardinality benchmarks, demonstrating that the smoothing improvement is orthogonal to both the weighting and structural axes of NB refinement. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Sec-tion 2 reviews related work; Section 3 develops the proposed estimators; Section 4 provides their theoretical analysis; Sec-tion 5 reports the empirical study and limitations; and Section 6 concludes the paper.

2. Related Work 

Smoothing for Categorical NB : The earliest smoothers for NB are Laplace’s add-one rule, Lidstone’s α-smoothing (Lid-stone, 1920), and the universal KT mixture (Krichevsky and Trofimov, 1981). Cestnik’s m-estimate (Cestnik, 1990) intro-duces a single concentration parameter and a fixed prior mean, but the concentration must be set manually. Good–Turing smoothing (Good, 1953) has been used for NB on text but, to our knowledge, without a finite-sample analysis. None of these methods adapt the smoothing strength per (class, fea-ture) inside the NB classifier. The closest precedent is the HPB procedure of Jambeiro Filho and Wainer (2008), which intro-duces Type-II hyperparameter selection for Bayesian-network nodes with high-cardinality parents. Our work di ff ers from HPB in two specific respects: ( i) HPB targets the more gen-eral high-cardinality-parent setting and reports no excess-risk or minimax-rate analysis, whereas we instantiate Type-II ML di-rectly in plain NB and provide non-asymptotic risk and calibra-tion bounds (Theorems 1–3); ( ii ) the HPB analysis predates the modern minimax-PMF-estimation literature (Han et al., 2015), from which our framing borrows. Empirical-Bayes TE (Micci-Barreca, 2001) pools categories toward the marginal mean but is used as a feature-engineering preprocess before logistic re-gression or trees, with no theoretical analysis. Outside NB, hi-erarchical Dirichlet smoothing is common in topic models (Blei et al., 2003) and language modelling (MacKay and Peto, 1995). 

Attribute and Instance Weighting : A large recent literature reweights NB factors using mutual information, gain ratio, or class-conditional correlation: CFW (Zhang et al., 2016), CAWNB (Jiang et al., 2019), CAVWNB (Zhang et al., 2020), 2AIWNB (Zhang et al., 2021), FTAWNB (Zhang and Jiang, 2022), ATFNB (Zhou et al., 2022), A 2WNB (Zhang et al., 2022), FNB (Hue and Boull´ e, 2024), and AG-NBC (He et al., 2025). These methods are orthogonal to ours: they reweight log ˆ P(X f | Y) without changing how the true probability is esti-mated. 

Structural Relaxation : AODE (Webb et al., 2005), tree aug-mented NB (Friedman et al., 1997), hidden NB (Jiang et al., 2009), and KDB (Sahami, 1996) relax the Naive independence assumption by adding parents to attributes. These methods re-tain Laplace-style smoothing internally and would also benefit from HEB. Recent vine-copula NB classifiers (S ¸ ahin and Joe, 2025) go further but at quadratic pair-copula cost and with-out consistency rates. Structural and smoothing improvements are orthogonal axes of NB refinement: HEB-NB advances the smoothing axis; AODE advances the structural axis. AODE has substantial memory overhead for high-cardinality features and is included in Section 5.3 as a reference baseline using a memory-capped variant. The combination of both axes is real-ized by our proposed HEB-AODE in Section 3.2. 

Calibration of NB : NB is famously over-confident (Niculescu-Mizil and Caruana, 2005); the standard correction is post-hoc Platt scaling (Platt et al., 1999), isotonic regression (Zadrozny and Elkan, 2002), or temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017). Moreover, P´ erez et al. (2024) propose a risk-based it-erative recalibrator. Our work is complementary: HEB-NB improves the upstream probability estimator, whereas calibra-tion corrects the downstream posterior. 

3. Proposed HEB-NB and HEB-AODE Estimators 

Figure 1 summarizes the training and prediction workflow of HEB-NB and its HEB-AODE extension. From the categorical training data, HEB-NB forms the class counts and the category-count vector for each class–feature pair, and constructs the feature-specific prior mean using either HEB-U (uniform) or HEB-M (pooled marginal). The concentration for each class– feature pair is learned by Type-II ML or set by the low-count fallback in Algorithm 1. The resulting empirical-Bayes poste-rior means are stored in log form with the estimated class pri-ors. At test time, NB log scores are normalized into posterior probabilities for evaluation, and prediction selects the class with the highest posterior probability. HEB-AODE applies the same smoother to AODE’s child-conditional factors, as detailed in Section 3.2.

3.2. The HEB-AODE Estimator 

Notably, smoothing and structural relaxations constitute or-thogonal axes for improving NB classifiers. While HEB-NB addresses the smoothing axis by introducing HEB estimation of class-conditional PMFs, the AODE (Webb et al., 2005) ad-dresses the structural axis by relaxing the conditional indepen-dence assumption. In this subsection, we demonstrate that the proposed HEB smoothing framework extends naturally to AODE, yielding a hierarchical variant referred to as HEB-AODE .

5. Experimental Results and Discussions 

The empirical study addresses four research questions: (Q1) Does HEB-NB attain Theorem 1’s ℓ1-rate empirically (Sec-tion 5.2); (Q2) Does HEB-NB significantly improve over fixed-smoother baselines under the Friedman–Nemenyi protocol, and how does it compare to a structural baseline like AODE (Sec-tion 5.3); (Q3) Does the smoothing improvement translate to better-calibrated probabilities (Section 5.4); (Q4) Is the result robust to the choice of fixed-α baseline and discretization gran-ularity (Section 5.5).

5.6. Discussion and Limitations 5.6.1. Continuous Features 

HEB-NB targets categorical NB. Continuous features are discretized by 10-bin quantile binning, as is standard in the NB-improvement literature (Yang and Webb, 2009); the discretiza-tion ablation in Section 5.5 confirms that the relative ordering of methods is stable across nbins ∈ { 5, 10 , 20 }. An extension to a Gaussian–Dirichlet mixture along the lines of John and Lang-ley (1995), in which continuous features keep a parametric fam-ily with hierarchically-pooled hyperparameters, is left to future work. 

5.6.2. Failure Modes and Accuracy-vs-Calibration Trade-o ff 

Type-II ML on the Dirichlet-multinomial marginal becomes unstable for classes with Nc < N0 = 10, where Algorithm 1 falls back to Laplace. A second failure mode is extreme out-of-vocabulary regimes (e.g., click-prediction), where HEB-NB’s calibration improvement persists but raw accuracy can lag Laplace’s depending on the imbalance structure. On click-prediction specifically, Laplace’s heavy uniform smoothing bi-ases predictions toward the majority class and achieves slightly higher accuracy than HEB-M alone (0 .808 versus 0 .792); HEB-M reduces log-loss by 17%, and the full HEB-M +GR com-bination recovers both (accuracy 0 .813, log-loss −29%, ECE 

−70%). 

5.6.3. Theoretical Scope and Future Directions 

Lemma 1 establishes a worst-case sanity bound, while the empirical advantage of HEB-NB at finite N stems from the unclamped Type-II ML maximiser’s √Nc log K f scaling (Fig-ure 5); a fully rigorous Type-II ML rate remains open. A central novelty, which is the matching lower bound for Laplace (The-orem 2) and the resulting risk-level strict separation (Corol-lary 1), addresses the long-standing question of whether adap-tive smoothing’s empirical improvements admit rigorous the-oretical justification, going beyond prior upper-bound com-parisons of Laplace /Lidstone /m-estimate /KT. When categories carry external hierarchical structure (ZIP codes, taxonomies, DNS hierarchy), the prior ¯ θ could borrow strength along the hierarchy rather than from a flat marginal pool, closely related to nested-Dirichlet language models (MacKay and Peto, 1995). 

6. Conclusion 

We introduced HEB-NB, a hierarchical empirical-Bayes smoothed Naive Bayes classifier whose per-(class, feature) Dirichlet concentration is learned by Type-II ML. Theoreti-cally, we proved a non-asymptotic ℓ1 upper bound (Theorem 1) matching the empirical-distribution minimax rate plus a vanish-ing data-adaptive bias, a matching Laplace-tight lower bound (Theorem 2), and a finite-sample risk-level strict separation (Corollary 1). A clamping-induced sanity bound (Lemma 1) guarantees asymptotic bias vanishing, with empirical diagnos-tics confirming the unclamped maximiser scales as √Nc log K f

(clamp binding on only 4.9% of pairs). We further derived a TV-tensorization excess-risk bound (Corollary 2) avoiding any lower bound on P(X), and a top-1 ECE bound (Theorem 3). The further introduction of HEB-AODE shows that the adap-tive smoothing transfers cleanly to structural NB relaxations. Empirically, on 31 UCI and OpenML datasets, HEB-NB attains the best mean Friedman rank on log-loss and Brier and signif-icantly dominates 13 of 24 baseline–metric pairs at α = 0.05. HEB-AODE significantly improves over vanilla AODE on F1, log-loss, and Brier ( p ≤ 0.029), and combining HEB-M with sqrt-MI weighting reduces top-1 ECE by 41%–70% on high-cardinality benchmarks, without any hyperparameter tuning. This work suggests several promising research directions for future work. First, extending the HEB hierarchical Type-II ML smoothing to other structural NB variants beyond AODE, e.g., tree augmented NB (Friedman et al., 1997), hidden NB (Jiang et al., 2009). Second, hierarchical-tree priors that exploit exter-nal category hierarchies. Furthermore, deriving finite-sample bounds on top-1 ECE for the HEB-NB plug-in classifier. Fi-nally, Gaussian–Dirichlet hierarchical mixtures for natively continuous features.
