## 摘要
理解极端事件的传播在许多经济和环境应用中非常重要，然而大多数计量经济学的因果推断方法关注平均效应而非尾部行为。本文研究极端事件中因果关系的识别，并推导相应的估计量及其渐近推断。作为变量极端实现之间因果依赖的度量，我们分析了带有重尾正则变化创新的线性结构因果模型中因果尾部系数（CTC）的渐近行为。与现有文献相比，我们允许系统中的变量具有异质尾部指数，并考虑潜在的重尾混杂因素。我们推导了理论结果，评估了这些条件下CTC的极限行为，并展示了尾部行为的差异如何有助于实现因果结构的识别。轻尾混杂因素渐近可忽略，但足够重的尾混杂因素可能诱发与直接因果效应在观测上不可区分的极端依赖模式。当存在合适的代理信息时，可以通过调整后的因果尾部系数恢复识别。基于这些结果，我们开发了在一般条件下极端事件因果关系的估计和推断程序。我们建立了所提出估计量的渐近性质，并推导了因果方向和重尾混杂的检验。模拟证据检验了它们的有限样本性能，并为其实施提供了指导。对气候和金融极端事件的应用说明了所提出的方法如何能够揭示那些可能被针对平均依赖的方法所遗漏的因果关系。

## Abstract
Understanding the propagation of extreme events is important in many economic and environmental applications, yet most econometric methods for causal inference focus on average effects rather than tail behavior. This paper studies the identification of causal relations in extremes and derives resulting estimators and their asymptotic inference. As measure of causal dependence between extreme realizations of variables, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of the Causal Tail Coefficient (CTC) within a linear structural causal model with heavy-tailed regularly varying innovations. In contrast to the existing literature, we allow the variables in the system to exhibit heterogeneous tail indices and consider the presence of potentially heavy-tailed confounders. We derive theoretical results assessing the limiting behavior of the CTC under these conditions and show how differences in tail behavior can help to reach identification of the causal structure. Light-tailed confounders are asymptotically negligible, but sufficiently heavy-tailed confounders can induce extremal dependence patterns that are observationally indistinguishable from direct causal effects. When suitable proxy information is available, identification can be recovered using an adjusted Causal Tail Coefficient. Based on these results, we develop estimation and inference procedures for causal relations in extremes under general conditions. We establish asymptotic properties of the proposed estimators and derive tests for the causal direction and heavy-tailed confounding. Simulation evidence examines their finite-sample performance and provides guidance on their implementation. Applications to climate and financial extremes illustrate how the proposed methods can uncover causal relations that may remain undetected by approaches targeting average dependence.
