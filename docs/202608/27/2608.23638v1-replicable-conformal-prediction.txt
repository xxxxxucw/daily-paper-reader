# REPLICABLE CONFORMAL PREDICTION 

Marios Papamichalis ∗ Regina Ruane † Theofanis Papamichalis ‡

## ABSTRACT 

Two analysts who calibrate a conformal predictor on independent samples deploy different set-valued classifiers with probability one, because the calibrated threshold is an order statistic of continuous scores. We ask when the identical classifier can be deployed, and at what cost. Exact agreement is impossible: a procedure that returns one fixed output with high probability cannot be uniformly valid, and with a shared seed an exactly replicable procedure must ignore its data. Rounding the threshold upward on a shared-seed random grid (replicable calibration, R ECAL )makes the deployed classifier identical across analysts with probability at least 1−ρ,preserves marginal coverage, keeps training-conditional coverage within ε of target, and needs O(κ2α(1 − α)/(ε2ρ2)) calibration points at miscoverage level α, where 

κ bounds the local score-density ratio. Lower bounds with explicit constants show the sample cost and a coverage inflation of order pα(1 − α)/n/ρ at calibration size n are unavoidable for any threshold calibrator; a consistent plug-in removes the factor κ2 asymptotically. Without any seed, a deterministic grid confines all analysts to two adjacent classifiers, optimally. Selecting the most favorable of M

recalibrations changes a R ECAL classifier with probability at most (M − 1) ρ; the same selection silently undercovers standard split conformal. Experiments on real ImageNet outputs, a four-hospital site split, four language-model families, and synthetic testbeds match the theory, including the predicted (ερ )−2 sample frontier. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Conformal prediction converts a trained model into a set-valued classifier with a finite-sample coverage guarantee (Vovk et al., 2005; Lei et al., 2018; Angelopoulos & Bates, 2023): a threshold 

eτ is calibrated on n held-out points, the classifier C(x) = {y : s(x, y ) ≤ eτ } is deployed, and the probability that the true label is covered is at least 1 − α. In deployment the calibrated classifier is an artifact: hashed, cached, versioned, audited. Three examples motivate this paper: two hospitals validate one frozen diagnostic model on their own held-out data before a joint deployment; a regulator re-runs a vendor’s calibration during an audit; a serving system caches conformal answer sets for language-model decoding (Quach et al., 2024). In each case the question is whether two independent calibrations produce the identical classifier, a stronger event than achieving similar coverage. With standard split conformal they never do. The calibrated threshold is an order statistic of continu-ously distributed scores, so two independent calibrations agree with probability zero (Proposition 1), and realized coverage fluctuates across calibration draws according to an exact Beta law (Vovk, 2012). The instability is exploitable: a party that redraws its calibration set M times and keeps the most favorable draw deploys sets that undercover by an amount on the order of √log M times the coverage standard deviation (Corollary 3), while the artifact passes any support check (Section 8, Figure 1b). A selective-inference failure at the deployment layer (Berk et al., 2013; Taylor & Tibshirani, 2015): candidate-wise validity does not survive selection (Fithian et al., 2014); replicability restores selection robustness (Corollary 3). Cross-sample instability is documented at benchmark scale (Prastalo & Jablonka, 2026); for prediction sets, built to be audited, it undermines that function.

A separate line of work formalizes agreement across independent samples. An algorithm is ρ-replicable if two runs on independent samples that share an internal random seed return the identical output with probability at least 1−ρ (Impagliazzo et al., 2022); the theory covers costs, list relaxations, and connections to privacy and stability (Bun et al., 2023; Dixon et al., 2023; Chase et al., 2023; Hopkins et al., 2024). To our knowledge the framework has not previously been applied to conformal prediction or distribution-free uncertainty quantification; this paper does so and characterizes the achievable trade-offs for conformal calibration. 

Questions. First, can any procedure return one fixed classifier with high probability and remain valid for every distribution? Second, we determine which relaxed agreements are achievable, at what sample and set-size cost, and whether that cost is necessary. Third, we quantify what replicable calibration guarantees against selective recalibration. 

Answers. Exact agreement is impossible in a strong sense: any procedure that outputs one fixed classifier with probability 1 − δ and is uniformly valid to within ε cannot exist when ε + δ < 

min {α, 1−α}; with a shared seed, exact replicability forces the output law to be the same under every distribution, so the deployed sets are uninformative (Theorem 1). Rounding the threshold upward on a random grid whose offset is generated from one shared seed (R ECAL ; Algorithm 1) makes the deployed classifier ρ-replicable, preserves the marginal guarantee, and keeps a two-sided training-conditional band, at sample complexity O(κ2α(1 − α)/(ε2ρ2)) under a local margin condition (Theorem 2). A direct argument shows n ≥ 916384 α(1 − α)/(ε2ρ2) is necessary for any threshold calibrator (Theorem 3), and the same machinery bounds the set-size cost: any replicable threshold calibrator inflates coverage by Ω( pα(1 − α)/n/ρ ), which R ECAL attains up to κ2 (Corollary 4); a consistent pilot removes the prior constant and the κ2 asymptotically (Proposition 3). Without a shared seed, single-answer replication is impossible below failure probability 1/2; a deterministic grid places every analyst, with probability 1 − δ, in a common two-element list of adjacent classifiers at rate O(κ2 log(1 /δ )/ε 2), optimal within the margin class (Theorem 4). 

What exact identity provides, and what it costs. Identity is the relevant event wherever artifacts are hashed, cached, or certified, and it bounds selection: with a pre-registered seed, M calibration redraws yield a second distinct classifier with probability at most min {1, (M − 1) ρ}, and expected coverage degrades by at most the same amount (Corollary 3); continuous stability notions such as one-point stability (Ndiaye, 2022) do not provide this. Identity has a cost beyond set size: R ECAL 

concentrates disagreement into rare full-grid-cell events, so on small-sample real data pointwise agreement can decrease while identity rises from 0 to 0.9; we therefore report a full spectrum of replication metrics (Definition 1).

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Replicability. Impagliazzo et al. (2022) introduced ρ-replicability with replicable statistical queries and a coin-problem lower bound of Ω(1 /ρ 2) at fixed tolerance; Bun et al. (2023) connected repli-cability, differential privacy, and perfect generalization (with an inherent quadratic overhead in one direction); Chase et al. (2023; 2024) tie replicability to global stability and Borsuk–Ulam obstructions underlying list-size lower bounds; Dixon et al. (2023) formalize list/certificate replicability; Hopkins et al. (2024) resolve the N -coin problem via isoperimetry; replicable bandits, RL, and testing followed (Karbasi et al., 2023; Eaton et al., 2026; Liu & Ye, 2024). Closest algorithmically is the replicable quantile of Kalavasis et al. (2024, Thm. B.6) ( eO(log 2 R/ (α2ρ2)) over an R-point grid via statistical-query (SQ) binary search); relative to it we contribute the conformal semantics (set-level identity with the finite-sample guarantee kept, two-sided coverage bands, unequal-n analysts), constants via the exact Beta law (no log 2 R; Remark 2), the coverage-metric lower bound, and the obliviousness/list theory; their construction is our distribution-free fallback (Proposition 5). 

Conformal prediction. Foundations and training-conditional behavior: Vovk et al. (2005); Lei et al. (2018); Vovk (2012); Bian & Barber (2023); APS/RAPS define the scores and evaluation we instrument (Romano et al., 2020; Angelopoulos et al., 2020); nested families (Gupta et al., 2022) coincide with our output class; conformal risk control (CRC) and conformal-LM stopping (Angelopoulos et al., 2024; Quach et al., 2024) calibrate one scalar, so R ECAL applies unchanged (we instantiate a risk-controlled variant in Section 8). One-point stability (Ndiaye, 2022) and e-value derandomization (Bashari et al., 2023) are within-run notions, orthogonal to cross-sample identity. Balinsky & Balinsky (2025) ask when one calibration set may be reused across predictions; we ask when two different sets yield one classifier. Scholten & G¨ unnemann (2025, App. C) make a single run deterministic via input-hash jitter; we adopt the device for tie-breaking (Remark 1) and show it leaves cross-sample identity at zero; post-selection inference conditions on the selection (Berk et al., 2013; Fithian et al., 2014; Taylor & Tibshirani, 2015), while Corollary 3 makes the selected object nearly deterministic, a complementary route (Table 1). Private prediction sets (Angelopoulos et al., 2022) privatize the same quantile; the generic DP-to-replicability conversion (Bun et al., 2023) costs quadratic overhead and a shared seed anyway. Prediction churn and multiplicity are documented deployment problems (Milani Fard et al., 2016; Marx et al., 2020; Prastalo & Jablonka, 2026); replicable calibration yields one verifiable classifier.

## 4 EXACT REPLICATION IS IMPOSSIBLE AND FORCES OBLIVIOUSNESS 

We first show that exact replication is incompatible with nontrivial validity, which motivates the (ε, ρ )

relaxation and the shared seed. Let Y be finite, |Y| = K ≥ 2, and Pac the class of distributions on Rd × Y with absolutely continuous X-marginal (labels arbitrary). Call a set-valued procedure 

uniformly ε-valid if PD,r, (X,Y )∼P [Y ∈ bC(X)] ∈ [1 − α − ε, 1 − α + ε] for every P ∈ P ac ; the two-sided requirement excludes the trivial bC ≡ Y .

Theorem 1 (Obliviousness of exact replication) . (a) If ε+δ < min( α, 1−α), no (1 , δ )-list-replicable procedure is uniformly ε-valid. Every seedless ρ-replicable procedure is (1 , ρ )-list replicable, so no seedless procedure is uniformly ε-valid and ρ-replicable when ε + ρ < min( α, 1 − α). (b) With a shared seed R, any exactly ( 0-)replicable procedure equals a fixed measurable v⋆(R) almost surely under every P ∈ P ac , so its output law is P -independent, and if it is also uniformly ε-valid, there is a fixed kernel p with p(x, y ) ∈ [1 − α − ε, 1 − α + ε] for a.e. x and every y, coverage under every P

equals EP [p(X, Y )] , and (1 − α − ε)K ≤ E| bC(x)| ≤ (1 − α + ε)K for a.e. x. (Proof: Appendix A.) 

Part (b) shows that an output law that does not vary with the sample cannot depend on the distri-bution, so its validity is vacuous; the exact endpoint is attained only by data-oblivious procedures (Appendix A’s example, exactly (1 , min {α, 1 − α})-list replicable at ε = 0 ). Determinism does not circumvent (a): sample randomness remains, and the same appendix gives a deterministic, uniformly 

0-valid procedure with atomless output law. Shared randomness is therefore necessary, and the following sections develop the (ε, ρ ) relaxation.

## 9 DISCUSSION , LIMITATIONS , AND FUTURE WORK 

Replicable calibration makes cross-analyst agreement checkable on the deployed artifact: one classifier or a certified pair, with matched sample and set-size costs and a selection bound. Three limitations point to future work. First, the real-image run uses cross-validated probability outputs (Northcutt et al., 2021), not one frozen network’s raw logits, and the clinical sites are small (n≤303 ); frozen production-scale deployment remains future work. Second, Proposition 3 removes 

ˆκ and κ2 asymptotically under continuity at the quantile; finite-n uniform removal is open. Third, Proposition 4 covers cutoff stabilization only; training-map and selection replicability remain open.

Remark 11 (R ECAL +, distribution-free fallback, extensions) . Calibrating at rank k+ = ⌈(1 − α +

en(δ))( n + 1) ⌉ gives F (τ ) ≥ 1 − α with probability 1 − δ whenever k+ ≤ n (training-conditional validity; Vovk, 2012; Bian & Barber, 2023). Without Assumption 1, replicable binary search over a b-bit score grid retains all guarantees at n = eO(log 2(R) log(1 /δ )/(ε2ρ2)) (Kalavasis et al., 2024, Thm. Every method calibrating one scalar over a nested family (Gupta et al., 2022) (APS, RAPS, conformal risk control, conformal-LM stopping) admits R ECAL unchanged; Section 8 instantiates R ECAL -APS, R ECAL -RAPS, and a risk-controlled top-p variant.
