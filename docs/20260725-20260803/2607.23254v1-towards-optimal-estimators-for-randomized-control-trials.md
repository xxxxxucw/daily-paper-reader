## 摘要
随机对照试验（RCT）是科技公司、制药研究和联邦机构进行因果推断的基础工具。虽然标准均值差估计量能提供无偏的处理效应估计，但其精度往往不足，尤其是在处理效应异质或结果呈现重尾分布的情况下。尽管存在众多提升精度的方法——从协变量调整技术到方差缩减策略——但近期研究表明，没有单一估计量能在所有数据集上表现最优。我们并非寻求针对单个随机对照试验的最佳估计量（这种做法可能因便利性选择而损害科学有效性），而是提出一个基于特定分析目标、在随机对照试验族中识别最优估计量的原则性框架。该方法利用样本拆分来估计随机对照试验族中评估指标（如均方误差、遗憾值）的分布，从而在保持渐近保证的同时，实现估计量之间的系统比较。我们使用亚马逊供应链优化技术试验样本和强化民主挑战数据集（25项干预措施）对该框架进行了演示。结果表明，最优估计量随分析目标的不同而显著变化：加权最小二乘在推断目标下表现最佳，而均值差在决策情境中使遗憾值最小化。这项研究为估计量选择提供了可操作的指导，同时保持了跨多样化研究应用的方法论严谨性。

## Abstract
Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are fundamental tools for causal inference across technology companies, pharmaceutical research, and federal agencies. While the standard difference-in-means estimator provides unbiased treatment effect estimates, it often lacks precision, particularly when treatment effects are heterogeneous or outcomes exhibit heavy-tailed distributions. Although numerous precision-enhancing methods exist---from covariate adjustment techniques to variance reduction strategies---recent research demonstrates that no single estimator performs optimally across all datasets. Rather than seeking the best estimator for individual RCTs, which risks compromising scientific validity through convenient selection, we propose a principled framework for identifying optimal estimators within families of RCTs based on specific analytical goals. Our approach uses sample splitting to estimate the distribution of evaluation metrics (e.g., mean squared error, regret) across RCT families, enabling systematic comparisons between estimators while maintaining asymptotic guarantees. We demonstrate this framework using a sample of Amazon's Supply Chain Optimization Technology trials and the Strengthening Democracy Challenge dataset (25 interventions). Results reveal that optimal estimators vary significantly by analytical objective: weighted least squares performs best for inference goals, while difference-in-means minimizes regret for decision-making contexts. This work provides actionable guidance for estimator selection while preserving methodological rigor across diverse research applications.
