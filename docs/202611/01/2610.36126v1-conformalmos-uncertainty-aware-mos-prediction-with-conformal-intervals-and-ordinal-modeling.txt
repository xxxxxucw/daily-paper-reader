# Abstract 

Accurate prediction of human Mean Opinion Scores (MOS) is crucial for evaluating synthetic speech quality in text-to-speech (TTS) and voice conversion (VC). Most MOS predictors out-put only point estimates and ignore uncertainty, limiting reli-ability in model selection and deployment. We propose Con-formalMOS, a framework that augments MOS prediction with conformal prediction to produce statistically valid prediction in-tervals with guaranteed coverage under exchangeability, along-side point estimates. During training, MOS labels are modeled in an ordinal-aware manner by converting one-hot targets into Gaussian-smoothed distributions, preserving score ordering. This improves uncertainty calibration, particularly when soft-max confidence is overconfident on out-of-distribution speech. On BVCC datasets, ConformalMOS achieves competitive accu-racy (MSE = 0.08) while delivering empirically validated cov-erage, enhancing reliability for real-world TTS and VC deploy-ment. 

Index Terms : Mean Opinion Score prediction, speech quality assessment, conformal prediction, uncertainty quantification, text-to-speech, voice conversion 

# 1. Introduction 

Accurately predicting human Mean Opinion Scores (MOS) is essential for evaluating the quality of synthetic speech and mu-sic. However, the task remains challenging due to the inher-ent subjectivity of human ratings and variability introduced by noise conditions, accents, and listener bias [1, 2]. Large-scale benchmarking studies show that even state-of-the-art systems achieve only moderate utterance-level correlation with human scores [3, 4], underscoring the difficulty of reliable automatic MOS estimation. Recent deep learning approaches leverage transfer learning and self-supervised representations to improve predictive accu-racy. For example, UTMOSv2 combines spectrogram-based image encoders with self-supervised speech embeddings [5], while waveform–spectrogram fusion and cross-modal encoders further enhance performance [6]. Despite these advances, exist-ing systems typically output only point estimates of MOS with-out quantifying predictive uncertainty. This limitation makes them vulnerable in real-world conditions where unseen acous-tic variations degrade reliability. As highlighted by [7], the lack of principled uncertainty modeling limits safe deployment in applications such as game development, movie production, and virtual assistants.

Although some probabilistic approaches attempt to model uncertainty [7, 6], they do not provide formal guarantees that predicted intervals contain the true MOS. Given the subjective and noisy nature of human ratings, reliable uncertainty estima-tion is critical for trustworthy deployment. To address this limitation, we introduce ConformalMOS, an uncertainty-aware MOS prediction framework built upon exist-ing MOS models [8, 9]. Our method integrates conformal pre-diction, a distribution-free framework that produces statistically valid prediction intervals with formal coverage guarantees un-der the assumption of exchangeability between calibration and test data [10]. Unlike heuristic uncertainty measures, conformal prediction offers theoretical reliability guarantees. We further incorporate ordinal-aware modeling to respect the discrete and ranked structure of MOS labels. Instead of treating MOS as purely categorical or continuous, we convert one-hot score labels into Gaussian-smoothed probability distri-butions over discrete score levels [11]. This captures the partial correctness of neighboring scores and better aligns the training objective with human rating behavior. The main contributions of this work are: • Conformal Uncertainty Intervals: Integration of conformal prediction to produce statistically valid MOS prediction inter-vals with formal coverage guarantees [10]. • Ordinal-Aware Modeling: Gaussian-smoothed label distri-butions that explicitly model MOS ordinality [11], improving point estimation accuracy. • Improved Accuracy and Robustness: On the BVCC dataset, our M2D2 backbone with α = 0.05 achieves a system-level MSE of 0.08, a 7.0% reduction from FUSE-MOS [6], while providing calibrated uncertainty for low-confidence and out-of-distribution inputs. 

# 2. Methodology 

The proposed framework estimates Mean Opinion Scores (MOS) by combining ordinal regression with uncertainty quan-tification through split conformal calibration. Pretrained au-dio representations are passed to an ordinal-aware prediction head that outputs a probability distribution over evenly spaced MOS bins. These probabilities are aggregated to produce a scalar MOS estimate. During training, Gaussian-smoothed or-dinal targets enforce the ranked structure of MOS labels. In a separate calibration stage, split conformal prediction computes a scalar half-width from calibration residuals, yielding finite-sample valid prediction intervals after clipping to the MOS range (Figure 1). arXiv:2610.36126v1 [cs.SD] 26 Oct 2026 Figure 1: Overview of the proposed ConformalMOS framework. Finally, post-hoc conformal calibration generates statistically valid prediction intervals, providing reliability-aware predictions.

2.3. Conformal Calibration 

Point estimates alone do not reflect uncertainty. We there-fore apply split conformal prediction, which provides finite-sample coverage guarantees without distributional assumptions [15, 16]. The labeled data are divided into a training set and a calibration set (10%). After training, absolute residuals on the calibration set are computed, and their empirical (1 − α) quan-tile defines the half-width ˆq. For a new input, 

I(x) = [ˆ y(x) − ˆq, ˆy(x) + ˆ q] ∩ [1 , 5] . (6) These intervals satisfy P (Y ∈ I(X)) ≥ 1−α under exchange-ability. Larger calibration residuals produce wider intervals, signaling higher uncertainty or distribution shift. Conformal calibration is post-hoc and computationally effi-cient: residual computation scales linearly with calibration size, and inference requires only adding and subtracting ˆq from ˆy(x).This provides reliability-aware MOS prediction with minimal overhead. 

# 3. Experimental Setup 

3.1. Dataset Preparation 

In our experiments, we utilize the BVCC dataset [17] for train-ing, which comprises 7,106 English audio samples collected from 187 TTS and Voice Conversion (VC) systems included in previous challenges. Although the dataset contains a modest number of samples, it is widely used in speech MOS prediction research and provides diverse samples from multiple TTS and VC systems. Each audio clip is annotated by multiple human raters, ensuring high-quality labels, and such dataset sizes are standard in the field due to the cost and effort of collecting hu-man MOS annotations. For model training and evaluation, we split the BVCC dataset according to the standard partitions de-fined in the VoiceMOS Challenge: 1,066 samples are reserved for testing, and 1,066 samples for validation. Of the remaining 4,974 samples in the development pool, we reserved a calibra-tion subset of 497 samples (10%) for conformal interval estima-tion, leaving 4,477 samples for the final model training. 

3.2. Training Details 

MOS prediction models were trained on BVCC using NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPUs (24 GB) for up to 1000 epochs with early stopping (patience 20) monitored on validation loss; most runs stopped after 300–400 epochs. Batch size was 32 for training and 8 for validation/testing [18]. We used SGD with momen-tum (LR 1×10 −4, momentum 0.9, weight decay 1×10 −4) and froze the upstream encoder, updating only the prediction head. Checkpoints were saved at lowest validation loss. Training typ-ically took 2 hours per run, balancing stable convergence and generalization while preventing overfitting.

We evaluated multiple α values 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, and 0.2 to examine their impact on conformal prediction intervals for MOS estimation. The choice of α directly affects the width of the intervals and the associated coverage probability. Larger α

values produce narrower intervals (improved sharpness) but in-crease the risk of undercoverage, while smaller α values yield wider intervals to ensure higher nominal coverage. Selecting multiple levels allows us to study the trade-off between interval tightness and reliability, aiming for intervals that are both in-formative and statistically valid. Empirical studies have shown that appropriate selection of α is essential for controlling er-ror rates and ensuring valid coverage in conformal prediction [23]. Based on these considerations, we selected an α of 0.05 as our primary setting, corresponding to 95% nominal cover-age, which balances interval width with reliability while main-taining the best overall performance. This shows that the novel pipeline improves the reliability of predictions without degrad-ing point-estimation accuracy. At the utterance level, Confor-malMOS M2D2 maintains strong correlations close to UTMOS and FUSE-MOS, with only a slight increase in MSE. In con-trast, the ConformalMOS wav2vec models underperform no-tably across all metrics, indicating that the choice of backbone strongly affects performance under the conformal framework. Overall, these results highlight that conformal and ordinal pre-diction enhance system-level robustness, especially when com-bined with the M2D2 backbone. Our best-performing Confor-malMOS M2D2 α = 0 .05 configuration surpasses baselines at the system level, while retaining competitive utterance-level accuracy. This confirms that conformal prediction can be ef-fectively integrated into MOS estimation pipelines to improve prediction reliability without compromising prediction quality.

Figures 2a and 2b present the calibration performance of our Conformal-MOS models on the BVCC dataset. Ideally, empir-ical coverage should closely match the nominal coverage, cali-bration error should be small, and intervals should be as narrow (sharp) as possible while remaining well-calibrated. The M2D2 models exhibit consistently accurate coverage. Empirical cov-erage aligns closely with nominal targets at most α levels, with low calibration error, especially for the M2D2 backbone. As ex-pected, increasing α leads to narrower prediction intervals and improved sharpness. This demonstrates the expected coverage-sharpness trade-off and confirms that the conformal calibration procedure is functioning properly on this backbone. # 5. Conclusion 

We introduce ConformalMOS, a reliability-aware MOS pre-diction framework combining ordinal modeling with split con-formal calibration. Unlike standard regression producing only point estimates, ConformalMOS outputs prediction intervals with formal coverage guarantees, capturing the subjectivity and variability of human MOS ratings. Experiments on BVCC show improved system-level performance with competitive utterance-level accuracy. The M2D2 backbone yields well-calibrated in-tervals with empirical coverage matching nominal targets, while wav2vec shows wider intervals and higher calibration error, em-phasizing the need for strong base predictors. Analysis con-firms the coverage–sharpness trade-off: increasing α narrows intervals but reduces coverage. By integrating ordinal-aware training with conformal uncertainty, ConformalMOS enhances interpretability and reliability, offering a practical, theoretically grounded approach for trustworthy MOS prediction in real-world applications.
